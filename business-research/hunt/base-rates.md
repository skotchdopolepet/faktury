## 1. Model ranking by real-world base rate

The question: how likely is a 10–15 h/week person with €8.5k to reach **€500–3,000/month profit** within about 12 months?

The single most useful base-rate fact is an estimate derived from primary data. **On Etsy, €500/month profit roughly means being in the top ~5% of shops.**
- Etsy marketplace GMS in 2025 was **$10,460.7M**, spread across **5.6M active sellers** [primary, Etsy 10-K FY2025: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm]. That is a **mean of ~$1,868/yr ≈ $156/month in gross sales per active seller** (estimate: 10,460.7 / 5.6).
- A widely cited figure says the top 5% of shops take 43% of sales [secondary, https://www.printful.com/blog/etsy-statistics]. If that holds, the top-5% shops average about $16k/yr (≈ $1,340/month **gross**). The other 95% average about $1,120/yr (≈ $93/month gross). Estimate arithmetic: 0.43 × 10,460.7 / 280k shops, and 0.57 × 10,460.7 / 5.32M shops.
- At a 35–50% net margin, €500/month profit needs about €1,000–1,400/month gross. That is the **top-5% average**.

**Takeaway:** in any "list it and hope" marketplace model, the base rate is terrible. Models where the margin is **locked in at purchase** and **demand is proven by sold comps before you spend** do much better.

**Contrarian conclusion.** The founder already failed with "clever, novel" ideas. The best base rate here is the least glamorous model: **buy under-priced used goods in CZ that have visible sold comps in the EU and US, add testing/cleaning/photography, and sell cross-border.** It is not passive, but the evidence is strongest that it pays at €500–1,500/month from 10–15 h/week. The main thing capping income is sourcing, not demand.

## 2. Candidates

### Tested & lightly refurbished 35mm film cameras (CZ-sourced, sold to EU buyers)
- **What you sell / type:** physical (used, tested, cleaned). Point-and-shoots (Olympus Mju/Trip, Canon/Minolta/Pentax compacts), Japanese SLRs (Canon AE-1, Pentax K1000, Minolta X-700), and East-bloc bodies (Praktica, Zenit, Meopta Flexaret). Each is sold "tested, new light seals, sample scans". Work per unit: 30–60 min cleaning and testing. Replacing light seals is a well-documented DIY job.
- **Buyer & channel:** Gen-Z and millennial analog shooters in DE/NL/FR/IT (eBay.de, Etsy, Vinted). US buyers via Etsy only for items above about €150, because of US duty and prepay friction.
- **Price (€):** €60–150 for compacts and SLR kits. €150–400 for hyped compacts (Mju II, T-series).
- **Unit economics (estimate):** average sale €120; buy €45 (target untested or "pozůstalost" lots, not hyped single items); marketplace fees ~13% ≈ €16 (eBay.de commercial or Etsy fee levels, *not verified this session*); packaging €2; parts (seals, test batteries) €3; returns/defect reserve 5% ≈ €6. **Net ≈ €48 per unit** before income tax. Shipping is charged to the buyer at cost. Time ≈ 1.5 h per unit including sourcing.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Mju II sells for $250–750 on eBay and is "harder to find in the secondhand market" [https://www.fieldmag.com/articles/olympus-mju-ii-review-35mm-film-camera].
  - Mju I sells for €120–180 in 2025 (NL reseller) [https://dutchthrift.com/blogs/gear/olympus-mju-1-review-compact-35mm-classic-for-modern-creators].
  - Film-camera trade volume reportedly up 25% YoY. A typical US flip costs $10–40, resells for $75–350 and nets ~$80 after fees [secondary, https://cluzy.app/resale-value/film-cameras].
  - Japanese 1970–90 SLRs "selling at prices rivaling original MSRP" [same source].
  - Specialist refurb shops exist (e.g. https://filmcamerastore.co.uk/collections/olympus-mju).
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:**
  - Hyped models are **already priced near EU levels in CZ**. Mju II on Bazoš/Aukro is listed at **3,200–4,777 Kč (≈ €130–195)** [search summary of https://foto.bazos.cz/inzeraty/olympus-mju-ii/ and https://aukro.cz/lp/nejkvalitnejsi-olympus-mju-ii]. That is roughly half the low end of US eBay pricing.
  - The real asymmetry is "untested/unknown" household lots, less-hyped bodies, and the **tested-with-warranty premium** over "as-is" listings. eBay is full of "READ As-Is" Mju listings [https://www.ebay.com/itm/397104391593].
  - Supply is shrinking by definition, since no new units are made.
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:**
  - Czech-language access to Bazoš, Sbazar, Aukro, estate clearances (vyklízení) and flea markets that DE/US buyers can't easily use.
  - Lower local price levels.
  - Cheap Packeta/Zásilkovna shipping into DE/AT/SK/PL/HU.
  - Knowledge of East-bloc brands such as Meopta.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** €1,500–2,500 for rolling stock of 30–50 bodies, seal material, a basic light box, test film and a used scanner. 10–15 h/week.
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (€):** pessimistic **€250** (6 units, sourcing-starved) / realistic **€700–1,000** (15–20 units) / optimistic **€1,800** (35–40 units with a reliable lot supplier). All figures are estimates at €48 net per unit.
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:**
  - Registration: volná živnost "Výroba, obchod a služby neuvedené v přílohách 1–3" as vedlejší činnost, CZK 1,000 fee.
  - Consumer law: as a trader you owe the EU 14-day withdrawal right and the legal guarantee. For used goods, the Czech civil code allows shortening the guarantee to 12 months by agreement (*not verified this session*).
  - VAT: CZ registration threshold of CZK 2M turnover (*not verified this session*). If you register, the second-hand margin scheme (§90 ZDPH) applies. Margin-scheme goods fall outside the €10k OSS distance-sales rule (*verify with a tax advisor*).
  - GPSR (Reg. 2023/988) applies to second-hand consumer goods; antiques are excluded (*verify*).
  - **Germany-specific friction:**
    - Battery-powered cameras are EEE, and ElektroG/WEEE registration may be required when a used device is placed on the German market from abroad (*verify at stiftung-ear.de*). Fully mechanical bodies avoid this.
    - VerpackG/LUCID packaging registration applies to all shipments to German consumers.
  - DAC7: platforms report sellers with ≥30 sales or ≥€2,000 per year to the tax office. Declare income.
- **Key risks:** sourcing is the bottleneck. Hidden faults cause returns (shutter capping, dead electronics). Film-trend reversal (demand is fashion-driven). Hyped-model price softening. Lithium batteries must be removed before shipping.
- **Confidence: 3/5.** Unit economics and demand are well evidenced. I could not directly verify the CZ→EU price gap for non-hyped bodies (Bazoš and eBay.de fetches were blocked), or the German WEEE status. **Validation step:** buy 10 untested bodies for ≤€400 total and see if ≥7 sell within 45 days at ≥2× cost.

### Czechoslovak mid-century art glass & ceramics, curated vintage export
- **What you sell / type:** physical (vintage, ≥20 years old per Etsy's vintage rule). Sklo Union pressed glass, František Zemek "Rhapsody" and Mstisov/Rückl cased glass, Borské sklo, Ditmar Urbach and Jihokera ceramics. No refurbishing: clean, identify, photograph.
- **Buyer & channel:** MCM decor buyers in the US, DE, NL and UK. Etsy vintage for €40–200 pieces. Pamono/1stDibs for €250+ attributed pieces. eBay.de for volume.
- **Price (€):** most pieces €40–150. Attributed pieces €250–600.
- **Unit economics (estimate):**
  - Average sale €70, buy €12 (bazaar/flea/estate prices; *estimate, not verified*).
  - Etsy 6.5% transaction + ~4% + €0.30 payment + $0.20 listing ≈ €7.9 (*fee schedule not verified this session*).
  - Offsite Ads 15% on ~25% of orders ≈ €2.6 (optional under $10k/yr sales).
  - Fragile packaging €3, breakage/returns reserve 5% ≈ €3.5.
  - **Net ≈ €40 per item.** Time ≈ 1–1.5 h per item.
- **Demand evidence:**
  - Zemek Rhapsody vase listed at **€600** and Peceny/Sklo Union vase at **$290** on Pamono [https://www.pamono.eu/rhapsody-vase-attributed-to-frantisek-zemek-for-sklo-glass-factory-former-czechoslovakia-1960s, https://www.pamono.com/vintage-glass-vase-by-frantisek-peceny-for-sklo-union-czech-1970s].
  - 1stDibs carries a dedicated "Union Sklo" category with **46 listings** [https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/union-sklo/].
  - Vintage Czech pottery averages **$359** on 1stDibs [https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/vintage-czech-pottery/].
  - Etsy runs dedicated market pages for "Ditmar Urbach" and "Czech Ditmar Urbach" [https://www.etsy.com/market/ditmar_urbach].
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:**
  - Eastern European MCM is abundant because "there's been far lower demand for items from this era where they were made", while in the US and Western Europe "the category is picked over".
  - A regional Etsy shop sends ~90% of sales to the US [https://www.dwell.com/article/why-is-there-so-much-great-midcentury-modern-decor-from-eastern-europe-on-etsy-5736f7f0-e6f13504].
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:** the goods originate in CZ. Czech-language sourcing (Bazoš, estate clearances, "po babičce" lots). The founder can read makers' marks and Czech catalogues. Western buyers can't source locally.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** €1,000–2,000 for initial stock of 60–100 pieces, a photo setup and packing materials. 10–12 h/week, with sourcing on weekends.
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (€):** pessimistic **€200** / realistic **€500–900** (12–22 items) / optimistic **€1,500** (with some €300+ pieces on Pamono). All estimates.
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:**
  - Same živnost, withdrawal-right and DAC7 notes as above. The margin scheme fits well if VAT-registered.
  - Items older than 50 years with high value may touch cultural-goods export rules for shipments outside the EU (*low risk at these prices; verify for rare pieces*).
  - US shipments: US de minimis ended **29 Aug 2025**. Česká pošta resumed US parcels on **1 July 2026**, but the sender must pre-declare and prepay duty and fees via **Zonos Prepay** [https://www.ceskaposta.cz/-/obnoveni-podani-zasilek-do-usa-a-portorika]. Shipping a tapestry to the US is "now $40" [Dwell, above].
- **Key risks:** breakage in transit. US duty/prepay friction hits the largest buyer segment, so price EU-first. Attribution errors, since unmarked glass is often misattributed. Taste cycles. Sourcing competition from existing CZ Etsy dealers.
- **Confidence: 3/5.** Demand and price levels are evidenced on primary marketplaces. CZ buy-side prices and sell-through rates could not be verified (Etsy and Bazoš fetches were blocked). **Validation step:** list 20 pieces and track 60-day sell-through.

### "Ready-to-shoot" adapted vintage lens kits (Carl Zeiss Jena, Meopta, Helios) for mirrorless cameras
- **What you sell / type:** physical. A cleaned and tested M42/Exakta/Praktica-mount manual lens bundled with the correct mirrorless adapter (Sony E, Fuji X, Nikon Z, Canon RF), caps, and sample images and video showing the lens character. Work: 20–40 min per unit (clean, check haze and aperture, photograph).
- **Buyer & channel:** hybrid photo/video creators on a budget wanting "cinematic swirl/flare" (eBay.de, Etsy, Vinted, Kleinanzeigen).
- **Price (€):** €80–220 per kit.
- **Unit economics (estimate):** sale €110; lens buy €35 (*estimate*); adapter €10 (bought in bulk; China-sourced adapters now carry the EU €3/item duty); fees ~13% ≈ €14; packaging €2; reserve 5% ≈ €5.5. **Net ≈ €43 per kit.**
- **Demand evidence:** eBay pre-owned Helios lenses range **$65–485**, with typical Helios 44-2 listings at **$79–170**. Carl Zeiss Jena vintage lenses sell for **$98–225**, and there is active adapter-for-mirrorless demand [search summary of https://www.ebay.com/b/Helios-Fixed-Prime-Telephoto-Camera-Lenses/3323/bn_80583450 and https://www.ebay.com/b/Carl-Zeiss-Jena-Vintage-Camera-Lenses/101642/bn_159315].
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:** weak. The Helios 44 was "churned out in the millions" [https://vintagetechnologyarchive.com/camera/helios/44-2/], so the bare lens is abundant. The asymmetry is the **kit and proof premium**: a bare "untested" lens versus a tested, adapted, sample-shot kit. That premium is estimated, not measured.
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:** CZ and neighbouring former-GDR/Eastern European supply of CZJ, Pentacon and Meopta glass. Czech classifieds. Shared sourcing and photo workflow with the film-camera line.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** €800–1,500 for 20 lenses, 30 adapters and cleaning tools. It fits inside the film-camera hours (the same buyers and channels).
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (€):** pessimistic **€100** / realistic **€300–500** / optimistic **€900**. Best run as an add-on line to the film-camera business.
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:** manual lenses are not EEE, so there is no WEEE issue (unlike electronic cameras). GPSR trader info is required on listings. Some old lenses are thoriated (mildly radioactive), so disclose that.
- **Key risks:** a buyer can easily assemble the kit himself, so the premium may be small. Fungus and haze returns. Price softening after the 2020–22 adapted-lens boom (not verified with current trend data).
- **Confidence: 2/5.** Demand is evidenced. The spread over CZ buy prices and the kit premium are unverified.

### Own-design 3D-printed accessories for the analog-photo niche (film-scanning holders, lens caps/hoods, battery adapters)
- **What you sell / type:** physical, printed in-house. Examples: DSLR/mirrorless "camera-scanning" film holders for 35mm and 120, replacement caps and hoods for vintage lenses, and mercury-battery (PX625) adapter housings. Work: design once, then 10–15 min per unit for finishing and packing.
- **Buyer & channel:** the same analog-shooter audience as the two candidates above (Etsy, eBay.de, Printables/own shop). Can also be bundled with camera sales.
- **Price (€):** €12–45.
- **Unit economics (estimate):** sale €30; filament and parts €2; Etsy fees ≈ €3.5; packaging €1. **Net ≈ €23 per unit.** Capital: one Prusa-class printer, about €900–1,300 (*estimate, not verified*).
- **Demand evidence:** only model-level evidence. **"Most 3D print sellers on Etsy make under $100/year"**, but those selling "functional replacement parts, niche hobby tools" with **own designs** earn **£100–600/month net** [secondary, https://layermath.com/blog/how-profitable-is-selling-3d-prints]. I did not obtain niche-level sales counts for film-scanning holders; this is **the evidence gap**.
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:** not established this session.
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:** Prusa Research is Czech, so there is a local printer, filament and spare-parts ecosystem and fast service. Cross-selling to the camera-buyer base.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** €1,200–1,600. 3–5 h/week once designs exist.
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (€):** pessimistic **€0–50** / realistic **€150–300** / optimistic **€700**.
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:** GPSR applies to new consumer products. You need a manufacturer name and address on the product or packaging, a risk assessment and a product identifier. Avoid trademarked shapes or logos.
- **Key risks:** no demand proof at niche level. Easy to copy. STL piracy.
- **Confidence: 2/5.** Include only as a low-cost complement to the resale lines, not as a standalone bet.

### CLP-compliant, multi-language label packs & generator for small EU candle/soap/wax-melt makers
- **What you sell / type:** digital, possibly with printed sheets. Editable CLP hazard-label templates per common fragrance load, in CZ/SK/PL/DE/HU. Optional later: a small web generator that turns a supplier's SDS data into a correctly formatted label. The founder would do the one-off classification work.
- **Buyer & channel:** thousands of home-based candle and wax-melt makers who sell cross-border in the EU. Channels: Etsy digital, Czech/Polish maker Facebook groups, and wax/fragrance suppliers as affiliate partners.
- **Price (€):** €15–40 per template pack, or €5–9/month for a generator.
- **Unit economics:** near-zero marginal cost. Etsy digital fees ≈ 11% + fixed (*not verified this session*). Net ≈ €13–35 per pack (estimate).
- **Demand evidence (regulation-forced):**
  - CLP labelling is "a legal requirement for any scented candle if it contains fragrance", covering candles, wax melts, reed diffusers and room sprays. The rules "apply to small scale home-based suppliers in the same way" as to industry [https://www.hsa.ie/eng/publications_and_forms/publications/chemical_and_hazardous_substances/obligations_of_eu_candle_and_reed_diffuser_producers_under_reach_and_clp.pdf, https://echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/17240/candle_fact_sheet_en.pdf/8cb8384d-7366-bc8c-f530-16c7e32c2180?t=1722514514575].
  - GPSR (since Dec 2024) adds a documented risk assessment for home-fragrance makers [https://www.hooleybrown.com/blog-post/gpsr-regulations-for-home-fragrance-manufacturers].
  - At least five paid compliance vendors target exactly this group (Japag, Biorius, Ecomundo, Annel, Taobe), which shows money changing hands: https://www.japagregulatorysolutions.com/post/clp-labeling-guide-for-uk-eu-candle-and-home-fragrance-manufacturers, https://biorius.com/other-regulatory-services/eu-candle-regulations/, https://ecomundo.eu/en/blog/candle-compliance-sds-labeling-obligations, https://annelltd.com/candle-compliance-reach-clp-gpsr/.
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:** **unproven.** English-language help is plentiful, and fragrance suppliers often give CLP data free. The possible gap is in **CEE languages**: CLP labels must be in the official language of each member state where the product is sold (*CLP Art. 17(2), not re-verified this session*). A CZ maker selling to SK/PL/DE needs 3–4 label versions.
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:** Czech/Slovak language. Proximity to the CZ/PL maker communities. Software skill if the founder has it.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** €200–1,000. 40–80 h of one-off build, then 2–4 h/week.
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (€):** pessimistic **€0** / realistic **€100–300** / optimistic **€1,000+** (if a supplier partnership bundles it).
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:** a real liability risk if a label is wrong. You need a disclaimer, and the "buyer remains the responsible supplier" point must be clear. This is a product, not a service, as long as no custom per-client work is sold.
- **Key risks:** the market may be small in CEE. Suppliers may give it away. Requires CLP mixture-classification knowledge.
- **Confidence: 2/5.** The regulatory demand is real, but a supply gap was not demonstrated. It is on the list because it is the only digital idea here with **forced** demand, which beats the 5–10% digital-product base rate.

## Rejected

Each item gives the concrete reason with evidence.

1. **Dropshipping (China → EU/US, Shopify + Meta ads).**
   - Only **10–20% of dropshipping businesses are profitable in year 1**, and sustained net-profit success is estimated at **1–5%** [secondary, https://trueprofit.io/blog/dropshipping-success-rate, https://getcarro.com/blog/dropshipping-statistics].
   - The structural hit is regulation. The EU abolishes the €150 duty exemption and levies **€3 duty per item on low-value parcels from 1 July 2026**, plus a new handling fee [primary, https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2025/12/12/customs-council-agrees-to-levy-customs-duty-on-small-parcels-as-of-1-july-2026/, https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/eu-customs-reform_en].
   - The US ended de minimis for all postal parcels on **29 Aug 2025** [https://www.ceskaposta.cz/-/obnoveni-podani-zasilek-do-usa-a-portorika].
   - It has no edge and is ad-cost-driven.

2. **Generic print-on-demand t-shirts, mugs and posters (Redbubble, Merch, Etsy POD).**
   - Redbubble marketplace revenue fell **−19% in FY25**, driven by "deterioration in contribution from organic sources".
   - Articore offset part of the drop with **artist fees** and base-price increases [https://eightx.co/blog/asx-teardown-redbubble-2026, https://www.ragtrader.com.au/news/redbubble-parent-s-fy25-sales-down-amid-board-stoush].
   - It has **542k selling artists** [https://www.retailbiz.com.au/latest-news/articores-net-loss-widens-as-revenue-dips-in-fy25/]. Estimate: ~A$195M marketplace revenue / 542k ≈ **A$360 per artist per year of *platform* revenue**, so artist earnings are a fraction of that.
   - Infinite supply and no edge.

3. **Generic digital planners and printables on Etsy.**
   - About **1.6M Etsy results for "planner"**, with a typical price around **$12.86** [secondary, https://www.accio.com/business/etsy-digital-planner-sales-trend-statistics].
   - Gumroad median creator income is about **$72/month**, and **<5% make >$1k/month** [secondary, https://insightraider.com/en/state-of-gumroad-2026].
   - Zero marginal cost combined with AI-generated clones means price collapse.

4. **AI-generated / low-content KDP books (journals, puzzle books, colouring).**
   - Amazon capped uploads at **3 new titles per day per account** after the AI flood [https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/].
   - AI content must be disclosed.
   - From **10 June 2025** the royalty on paperbacks under ~$9.99 dropped from **60% to 50%**, which makes $5 journals unprofitable [search summary of https://www.univers.studio/blog/kdp-ai-content-policy-2026/].

5. **Amazon FBA private label in commodity categories.**
   - Roughly half of active sellers make ≤$1k/month [secondary summary of Jungle Scout 2025, https://amzprep.com/amazon-seller-profit/].
   - About **45% of new FBA sellers quit within a year**, and per-unit FBA costs are **20–30% higher than 2022**.
   - Example given: at $1.18 CPC and 5% conversion, ad cost is $23.60 per sale on a $35 product with a $12 pre-ad margin, which is "underwater" [secondary, https://www.businessradar.org/markets/amazon-fba-margin-squeeze-2026].
   - For an EU seller, add per-country packaging, WEEE and battery EPR and GPSR, plus €3–5k tied up in inventory.

6. **Generic scented candles and wax melts.**
   - CLP labels and SDS are mandatory for fragranced products, "in the same way" for home-based makers as for industry [https://www.hsa.ie/eng/publications_and_forms/publications/chemical_and_hazardous_substances/obligations_of_eu_candle_and_reed_diffuser_producers_under_reach_and_clp.pdf].
   - GPSR adds a lifecycle risk assessment [https://www.hooleybrown.com/blog-post/gpsr-regulations-for-home-fragrance-manufacturers].
   - The market is extremely crowded with hobbyists pricing below cost, and fragile, heavy shipping from CZ adds cost.
   - The only angle left is selling *to* candle makers (see the CLP-label candidate), not competing with them.

7. **NFTs and crypto digital collectibles.**
   - NFT volumes are **down >90% from 2021 highs**.
   - Market capitalisation fell from ~$20B (early 2022) to **~$4.9B by Oct 2025**.
   - OpenSea pivoted to a general crypto aggregator, and Blur fell from >$1B per month to **$92M** [https://cryptonews.com/news/opensea-reinvents-itself-as-crypto-aggregator-amid-90-nft-volume-crash/, https://coinlaw.io/nft-market-growth-statistics/].
   - A decaying fad.

8. **Reselling free or popular STL 3D prints (articulated dragons, low-poly Pokémon, fidget toys).**
   - "Most 3D print sellers on Etsy make under $100/year". Generic articulated dragons and low-poly Pokémon are named as saturated [secondary, https://layermath.com/blog/how-profitable-is-selling-3d-prints].
   - Licensed characters also carry IP takedown risk.

9. **Vinted fast-fashion clothing flipping as the main engine.**
   - Net per item is small (estimate €5–15), so €500/month needs 50–100 sold items. At 10–15 h/week that is labour-bound. Industry estimates put typical part-time net at **$200–500/month** [secondary, https://www.underpriced.app/blog/how-much-money-can-you-make-reselling-realistic-income-2026].
   - DAC7 reporting kicks in at ≥30 sales or ≥€2,000 per year (*not verified this session*).
   - Private Vinted accounts are not meant for trading.
   - Fine as a disposal channel, but not as the business.

10. **Arbitraging *hyped* film cameras bought at CZ asking prices (e.g. Mju II).**
    - CZ Bazoš/Aukro asks are already **3,200–4,777 Kč (≈ €130–195)** [search summary of https://foto.bazos.cz/inzeraty/olympus-mju-ii/].
    - After ~13% fees, testing film, the returns reserve and possible WEEE/packaging registration in DE, the spread to EU sell prices is thin.
    - This is a sub-trap inside candidate 1: buy untested lots and non-hyped bodies instead.

11. **Generic micro-SaaS or "passive" info products without a forced-demand hook.**
    - About 70% of micro-SaaS stay under $1k MRR, and part-timers grow 2.2× slower [secondary, https://saasranger.com/blog/micro-saas-revenue-reality-what-1000-founders-actually-earn/].
    - The founder's history of "clever ideas that didn't sell" fits this failure mode exactly.
    - Only pursue it with a regulation or a mandatory workflow behind the demand.
