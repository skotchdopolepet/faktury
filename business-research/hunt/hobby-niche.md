## Candidates (ranked)

### 1. Kydex IWB/AIWB holsters for Czech-made pistols (CZ P-10 family, Shadow 2, CZ 75 Compact), including weapon-light variants
- **What you sell / type:** Physical. Heat-formed Kydex/Boltaron holsters made to order in a small range: IWB, AIWB and OWB, left and right hand, 3–4 colours, with optional light-bearing versions for common lights. A later add-on on the same tooling is Kydex knife sheaths for hunting and EDC knives. Moulding, trimming, sanding and assembly take about 45–75 min per unit (estimate, based on the typical make steps).
- **Buyer & channel:** Czech concealed-carry licence holders and sport shooters. Sales go through an own Shoptet e-shop, Czech gun forums and Facebook groups, and wholesale or consignment to gun shops. RH Holsters, for comparison, is stocked by Armed.cz and Sullyho zbrojnice. Later: SK, and PL via Allegro.pl (PL demand unverified).
- **Price (€):** Target of 1,590–1,990 CZK (about €63–79). **This is an estimate:** I could not open competitor price lists (kydexovapouzdra.cz and the CZUB store were blocked). Verify it against RH Holsters, kydexovapouzdra.cz, OnGear and CZUB "Sharky".
- **Unit economics (estimate):** Kydex sheet share €4–7, clip or loops €3–10, screws and eyelets €1, packaging €0.5, so materials come to about **€9–18**. Payment fees are about 2% (€1.5). Shipping goes via Zásilkovna at €3–4, passed to the buyer. Allow 3–5% returns, because standard-model holsters are not "made to consumer specifications", so the 14-day withdrawal right applies (Directive 2011/83/EU Art. 16(c), background: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj). **Net before labour is about €45–60 per holster**, which works out to roughly €40–55 per hour of making time.
- **Demand evidence (numbers + URLs):**
  - Czech firearm-licence holders reached **320,888 in Oct 2025**, and **Nov 2025 set a modern-era record** above the 2001 peak: https://zbrojnice.com/2025/12/12/v-listopadu-2025-drzelo-zbrojni-prukaz-nejvice-osob-v-historii-moderni-cr-rekord-z-2001-prekrocen/
  - The number grew by **about 5,000 in 2025**, with a rush of applicants before the legislation change: https://zbrojnice.com/2026/01/11/zbranove-statistiky-2025-prekotny-zajem-o-zbrojni-prukazy-pred-zmenou-legislativy/
  - In Kraj Vysočina, **68.6% of licence holders (11,988 people) hold group E**, the concealed-carry-for-self-defence category. This is a regional figure; the national share was not verified. https://policie.gov.cz/clanek/krajske-reditelstvi-policie-kvs-zpravodajstvi-pocet-drzitelu-zbrojnich-prukazu-roste.aspx
  - Česká zbrojovka's own company store sells Kydex IWB holsters for the P-10 C ("Sharky"), which shows the OEM sees paying demand: https://prodejna.czub.cz/Vnitrni-kydexove-pouzdro-IWB-Sharky-pro-CZ-P-10-PRAVE-d1939.htm
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:**
  - Custom holsters at kydexovapouzdra.cz ship in **4–8 weeks** for items not in stock (https://www.kydexovapouzdra.cz/kydexova-pouzdra-na-zbran-cz-p-10-c/), and **4–9 weeks** for custom C/IWB (https://www.kydexovapouzdra.cz/c-iwb-glock-vnitrni-kydexove-pouzdro-na-zakazku/).
  - RH Holsters quotes **2–5 weeks "depending on workload"**: https://www.sullyhozbrojnice.cz/pouzdra-na-zbrane/rhh-kydexove-pouzdro-na-miru/
  - Multi-week backlogs at the established makers mean supply lags demand. The gap is moderate, not dramatic, since buyers do eventually get served.
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:** The home market is one of the EU's largest carry cultures. CZ pistols are made domestically, which gives easy access to fit-check guns via friends, ranges and shops. Czech-language customer service and 1–3 day domestic delivery compete directly against 2–9 week queues. Lead time is the whole pitch: "in stock, ships in 48 h" for the ~6 most popular models.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** About **€1,500–3,500 (estimate)**: toaster oven €80–150, foam press €100, bandsaw or jigsaw €150–300, belt/spindle sander €200–400, drill press €200, router with flush bit €150, Kydex stock €300, hardware €300, and moulds. Moulds are either 6 blue guns at about €100 each, or a 3D printer (€300–600) printing gun-model moulds. E-shop and photos about €300. **10–15 h/week**, which caps output at about 30–40 holsters/month after admin and marketing time (estimate).
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (estimate):** pessimistic **€100** (about 3 units), realistic **€700** (about 15 units × €47), optimistic **€1,600** (about 32 units × €50, near the time cap).
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:**
  - A holster is not a firearm or firearm part, so no arms licence is needed to make or sell one (background, verify). Owning real guns for fitting needs a licence; blue-gun or 3D-printed moulds avoid that.
  - The new firearms act takes effect in 2026 (Act 90/2024 Coll., background). The June 2026 statistics already speak of "zbrojní oprávnění": https://zbrojnice.com/2026/07/13/zbranove-statistiky-vi-26-pokles-poctu-drzitelu-zbrojnich-opravneni/
  - The General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR, 2023/988) applies: traceability, product safety info and a responsible person (background: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj).
  - A free trade licence (volná živnost) is enough, and the business can run as vedlejší činnost (background).
  - Ad platforms restrict weapon-related ads (background, verify).
- **Key risks:**
  - Strong incumbents: RH Holsters, kydexovapouzdra.cz, OnGear (https://ongear.cz/zakazkova-vyroba-kydexovych-pouzder/), CZUB's own line, plus shops like Kentaur (https://www.kentaurzbrane.cz/en/kydexova-pouzdra/).
  - The **June 2026 statistics headline reports a decline in licence holders** after the law change (URL above), so growth may have peaked.
  - Product-liability exposure if retention fails.
  - Every new pistol model needs a new mould.
- **Confidence: 3/5.** The customer base is large and documented and the backlogs show some excess demand, but I could not verify competitor prices or any sales counts, and the market already has several capable makers.

### 2. Lead-free sinkers and small weights, positioned for the 2026 EU REACH lead-in-fishing-tackle sales ban
- **What you sell / type:** Physical. Lead-free split shot, drop-shot and inline weights, and jig heads of 50 g or less. They are either hand-cast from a low-melting tin–bismuth alloy in aluminium moulds, or private-labelled from steel or tungsten sourced in China and then graded, coated and packed in CZ. Carp leads over 50 g would follow later. Casting and packing take about 1–2 h per batch of 50–100 packs (estimate).
- **Buyer & channel:** Wholesale to CZ, SK and PL tackle shops that must clear lead stock. Retail on Allegro.cz/pl, eBay.de and Amazon.de.
- **Price (€):** €4–8 per retail pack or assortment (estimate). Lead-free products currently sell at a premium to lead (background, unverified).
- **Unit economics (estimate):** Cast Bi-Sn packs cost about €1–2 in metal and packaging per pack (alloy price unverified; get a quote before committing). Marketplace fees of 10–15% take about €0.6–1. The wholesale price would be about 50% of retail. **Net is roughly €1–3 per pack.** This is a volume game, so it only works through shop wholesale.
- **Demand evidence (numbers + URLs):**
  - Member states in the REACH Committee **adopted the Commission's restriction on 29 April 2026** by a small majority. The ban covers **placing on the market (sales)**; the recreational-use ban was dropped. https://www.eftta.com/news/article/reach-committee-adopts-eu-lead-restrictions-for-fishing-tackle · https://angling-international.com/2026/05/03/lead-ban-restrictions-to-effect-sales-not-use-by-anglers/ · https://www.birdlife.org/news/2026/04/30/press-release-eu-takes-major-step-toward-lead-ban-in-fishing-weights/
  - Transition periods: **lead wire and "drop-in" sinkers are banned from sale after 6 months; sinkers and lures ≤50 g after 3 years; >50 g up to 1 kg after 5 years.** A 3-month scrutiny period by the Parliament and Council follows adoption, so the rule could enter into force in late 2026. The WTO notification was filed on 17 Sep 2025. https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2025/09/safeguards-14525-eu-consults-over-lead-in-fishing-tackle-under-reach
- **Supply gap / asymmetry evidence:** A regulation-forced substitution across the whole EU tackle market. **No current shortage is evidenced.** The gap appears only when each deadline hits: about Q2 2027 for the 6-month category, about 2029 for ≤50 g. I could not verify the exact scope of "drop-in sinkers"; read the final legal text first.
- **Edge for a CZ-based solo founder:** Weak to modest. CZ has a large carp and coarse angling culture (size unverified in this run). There is early-mover access to CZ tackle-shop owners and cheap local casting. The real "edge" is timing, not a structural advantage.
- **Startup cost (€) & hours/week:** €800–2,000 (estimate): melting pot and moulds €200–400, alloy stock €300–800, packaging and labels €200, GPSR labelling and test samples €200. 5–10 h/week.
- **Realistic monthly profit after 12 months (estimate):** pessimistic **€0**, realistic **€150**, optimistic **€600**. Twelve months out (Sep 2027), only the 6-month category is in force. The main ≤50 g wave arrives around 2029.
- **CZ/EU legal/regulatory notes:** REACH restriction (URLs above). Lead-free alloys must not contain other restricted substances (for example cadmium). GPSR applies. Casting at home is a free trade; check building fire rules for melting metal (background).
- **Key risks:**
  - The final text may still change.
  - Big brands and Chinese tungsten will flood the category.
  - There is no use ban, so anglers can hoard lead.
  - Unit value is low relative to shipping costs.
  - The founder's 12-month window is before most of the demand exists.
- **Confidence: 2/5.** The regulatory trigger is real and documented. The timing and the solo edge are weak.

## Live animals and plants: CZ/EU rules (background; I could not verify online in this run)
- **Plants, including aquarium and terrarium plants:** Plants for planting moved between professional operators in the EU need a **plant passport**, and the seller must register with ÚKZÚZ as a professional operator. Exports to the UK, CH or the US need a phytosanitary certificate. Reg. (EU) 2016/2031: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/2031/oj
- **CITES:** The EU wildlife-trade regulation (EC) 338/97 applies to many reptiles, some corals, orchids and cacti. Captive-bred Annex B species need proof of origin: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/1997/338/oj
- **Invasive species:** Some aquarium favourites are on the Union IAS list and **may not be sold at all**, for example marbled crayfish and red swamp crayfish. Reg. (EU) 1143/2014: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/1143/oj
- **Live-animal transport:** Council Reg. (EC) 1/2005 applies to vertebrates moved "in connection with an economic activity", which covers selling reptiles or fish by post. Invertebrates such as shrimp, isopods and springtails fall outside it, but carrier terms often prohibit live animals: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2005/1/oj
- **Net effect for this lens:** Physical *non-living* products (holsters, weights, model parts, antler chews) avoid most of this. That is part of why they rank above the live-stock ideas.
