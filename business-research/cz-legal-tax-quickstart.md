# Czech side-business legal and tax quickstart (2026)

For a Czech tax resident in full-time employment who wants to sell physical and/or digital products online as a side business (vedlejší samostatná výdělečná činnost, "vedlejší SVČ") to CZ, EU and worldwide buyers.

Status date: **2026-09-29**. This is a practical orientation, not legal or tax advice. Before the first sale, spend one paid hour with a Czech tax advisor (daňový poradce) to confirm the VAT points in section 3.

## 1. Trade licence (živnostenský list)

### 1.1 Which licence type

**Free trade (volná živnost)**, officially "Production, trade and services not listed in Annexes 1–3 of the Trade Licensing Act" (Výroba, obchod a služby neuvedené v přílohách 1 až 3 živnostenského zákona). No qualification is needed. **One licence covers up to 80 fields of activity (obory činnosti)**, and you tick the ones you need [V] ([BusinessInfo list of fields](https://www.businessinfo.cz/formulare/priloha-seznam-oboru-cinnosti-nalezejicich-do/), [MPO JRF list of fields, PDF](https://mpo.gov.cz/assets/cz/podnikani/zivnostenske-podnikani/crm-jednotny-registracni-formular/2025/2/obory.pdf)). Fields relevant to this project:

| What you do | Field(s) to tick (obor činnosti) | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Reselling goods online, including your own products | Wholesale and retail (Velkoobchod a maloobchod) | [V] field exists |
| 3D printing plastic products | Production of plastic and rubber products (Výroba plastových a pryžových výrobků). Its official scope covers plastic household goods, machine parts shaped from plastics, and similar items | [V] ([BusinessInfo field contents](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/obsahova-napln-zivnosti-volne-1/)) |
| Software, apps, web tools, SaaS | Software provision, IT consultancy, data processing, hosting and web portals (Poskytování software, poradenství v oblasti informačních technologií, zpracování dat, hostingové a související činnosti a webové portály) | [V] field exists |
| E-books, printables, templates, digital art | Publishing, printing, bookbinding and copying (Nakladatelské činnosti, polygrafická výroba, knihařské a kopírovací práce), plus the software field above for templates or code | [B] |
| Costume jewelry from base metals (not precious metals) | A volná field. BusinessInfo states that costume jewelry from common metals is volná, while precious metals are řemeslná (see below) | [V] ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/zlatnictvi-a-klenotnictvi-2/)) |
| Low-voltage electronics assembly (USB or battery gadgets) | Production of electronic components and electrical equipment (Výroba elektronických součástek, elektrických zařízení a výroba a opravy elektrických strojů, přístrojů a elektronických a telekomunikačních zařízení) | [B] |

Tip: tick every field you might plausibly use now. Adding fields to an existing volná živnost later is a simple change notification [B].

### 1.2 When a craft (řemeslná) or bound (vázaná) licence is needed

A craft or bound licence requires proof of competence. Options are an apprenticeship certificate or other relevant education (výuční list or vzdělání), roughly **6 years of practice** in the field, or appointing a **responsible representative (odpovědný zástupce)**, who can act for at most 4 businesses [V] ([Firmin: řemeslné živnosti](https://www.firmin.cz/zivnosti/remeslne), [Dostupný advokát](https://dostupnyadvokat.cz/blog/remeslna-zivnost)).

| Product | Licence | Notes | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jewelry from **gold/silver/platinum**, setting stones | **Řemeslná: Goldsmithing and jewelry making (Zlatnictví a klenotnictví)** | Base-metal costume jewelry is volná. Workaround: have a licensed workshop (e.g. in Turnov or Jablonec) make the pieces and sell them yourself under "Velkoobchod a maloobchod". Hallmarking still applies (4.8) | [V] licence; [B] workaround |
| **Food** | Some are řemeslné: bakery and confectionery (Pekařství, cukrářství), butchery (Řeznictví a uzenářství), dairy (Mlékárenství), milling (Mlynářství), brewing (Pivovarnictví a sladovnictví). Other food is under the volná field Production of food and starch products (Výroba potravinářských a škrobárenských výrobků) | You also need food-business registration (4.7) | [B] |
| **Cosmetics** (soaps, balms, creams, beard oil) | **Vázaná: Production of cosmetic products (Výroba kosmetických prostředků)**. Requires a relevant university education (e.g. chemistry, pharmacy) or an odpovědný zástupce | Plus CPNP and a safety report (4.7) | [B] |
| **Electrical work** on mains-voltage (230 V) equipment or installations | **Vázaná: Installation, repair, inspection and testing of electrical equipment (Montáž, opravy, revize a zkoušky elektrických zařízení)**. Requires Czech electrical qualification | Selling finished CE-marked electronics or making low-voltage gadgets falls under volná fields. Refurbishing mains devices is a grey zone, so ask the trade office | [B] |

### 1.3 Cost and process (about 1 day of effort, 5 working days to register)

1. **Online:** go to **Portál živnostníka / RŽP, Unified Registration Form (Jednotný registrační formulář, JRF)** at [rzp.gov.cz](https://rzp.gov.cz/epo-jrf/cs/o-sluzbe). Log in with **Identita občana** (bank ID, eObčanka, or the Czech state's "NIA ID" app), or send the form from a data box (datová schránka) [V].
2. Fee: **800 CZK** online. That is 1,000 CZK minus the 20% discount for an interactive e-form under § 9 of Act 634/2004 Sb. (the discount came in with Act 12/2020 Sb.). Paper filing at any trade office (živnostenský úřad) or Czech POINT costs **1,000 CZK**. Each further notification costs 500 CZK (400 CZK online) [V] ([finance.cz](https://firmy.finance.cz/545382-ohlaseni-zivnosti-elektronicky-uspora-datova-schranka/), [Praha 17 fees](https://www.repy.cz/mesto-a-urad/urad-mestske-casti-praha-17/odbory-a-oddeleni/odbor-zivnostensky/spravni-poplatky-pri), [RŽP guide](https://rzp.gov.cz/pruvodce-podnikanim/cs/164,260.html)).
3. The office enters you in the Trade Register **within 5 working days**, and you get an **IČO** (business ID) [V].
4. The **same JRF** can carry your registration with the tax office (finanční úřad), your start notice to ČSSZ (social insurance) and your start notice to your health insurer. Tick those sections so you file once [B]. Deadlines if you file them separately: health insurer within 8 days of starting; ČSSZ by the 8th day of the month after you start. These notices apply to side activities too [B].
5. A **business data box (datová schránka FO podnikající)** is set up automatically [B]. Check it weekly: a document counts as delivered 10 days after it lands, whether you log in or not. Once you have a data box, tax filings must be electronic, which gives you the later May deadline.
6. **Employment contract check:** under § 304 of the Labour Code (zákoník práce), a side business **in the same line of business as your employer** needs the employer's prior written consent [B]. Unrelated product sales need nothing.

### 1.4 Selling your own used items vs. running a business

- **Selling your own used personal items** (clothes, toys, a bike, furniture) is **exempt from income tax and is not declared, even if the platform reports you under DAC7** [V] ([Finanční správa FAQ: income from second-hand sales](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/dotazy-a-odpovedi/dan-z-prijmu-fyzickych-osob/prijmy-z-bazaroveho-prodeje), [Taxorio](https://www.taxorio.cz/blog/dane-z-prodeje-vinted-bazos-dac7-2026)).
- **Buying items in order to resell them repeatedly for profit** is business. It needs a živnost and is taxable as self-employment income. What counts is the character of the activity, not the number of pieces [V] (same sources). Occasional income that is not business is exempt up to **50,000 CZK/yr** (raised from 30,000 CZK in 2024) [V] ([Fakturoid](https://www.fakturoid.cz/almanach/dane/prilezitostny-prijem)). **Making and repeatedly selling products is not "occasional".**
- **Risks of unlicensed trading:** assessment of back tax plus social and health insurance with penalties and interest; an administrative fine under the Trade Licensing Act for trading without a licence (up to CZK 1 million) [B]; and possibly the criminal offence of unauthorised business (neoprávněné podnikání, § 251 of the Criminal Code) at larger scale [B]. Consumer-law exposure also applies: a trader posing as a private seller commits an unfair commercial practice [B]. Detection is now easy because platforms report sellers under **DAC7** (5.1).

## 2. The side activity while employed (vedlejší SVČ)

Self-employment counts as a **side** activity when, for example, you are employed and participate in sickness insurance, as any normal full-time job does. It also counts as a side activity for people who receive a pension, parental benefit or certain other benefits, or who are full-time students [V] ([ČSSZ: OSVČ v kostce](https://www.cssz.gov.cz/osvc-v-kostce), [ČSSZ: compulsory participation](https://www.cssz.cz/duchodove-pojisteni-osvc-povinna-ucast)).

### 2.1 Social insurance (sociální pojištění, ČSSZ)

- **2026 threshold (rozhodná částka): 117,521 CZK of profit** (daňový základ z podnikání). The formula is (2.4 × 46,278) × 1.0581. If your profit for the year is **at or below** the threshold, **no social insurance is due** for 2026 [V] ([ČSSZ](https://www.cssz.gov.cz/osvc-v-kostce), [Jaknaico](https://jaknaico.cz/vedlejsi-cinnost/), [Vyfakturuj](https://www.vyfakturuj.cz/fakturopedie/osvc-jako-vedlejsi-cinnost/)).
- **Part year:** deduct **9,794 CZK for each calendar month without activity** [V]. **Timing matters in 2026:** if you register in October 2026, the 2026 threshold drops to 117,521 − 9 × 9,794 = **29,375 CZK** (computed). If Q4 2026 profit would exceed that, consider starting on 1 January 2027 instead. The **2027 threshold** is set by government regulation each autumn, so check cssz.cz in Oct–Nov 2026.
- **Above the threshold, it is a cliff, not a marginal charge.** You pay 29.2% × 55% of the **whole** profit, about **16.06% of profit** [V 55% base, e15/ČSSZ; B 29.2%]. Crossing the threshold by 1 CZK costs about **18,875 CZK** (computed: 29.2% × 55% × 117,522). Manage profit near the threshold, for example through the timing of legitimate expenses.
- **Advances:** none in the first year. From the following year, monthly advances are based on the previous year's profit, with a **minimum of 1,574 CZK/month** in 2026 for side activities, and only if you were compulsorily insured [V] ([ČSSZ 2026 overview](https://www.cssz.gov.cz/-/prehled-nejdulezitejsich-udaju-pro-socialni-zabezpeceni-v-roce-2026), [ČSOB guide](https://www.pruvodcepodnikanim.cz/clanek/minimalni-zalohy-osvc-2026/)). Sickness insurance for the self-employed is voluntary (minimum 243 CZK/month in 2026) and pointless while employed [V amount].
- **Annual statement (Přehled o příjmech a výdajích OSVČ)** to ČSSZ: due **within 1 month after your tax-return deadline**. File it every year you were registered, even if you owe nothing [B].

### 2.2 Health insurance (zdravotní pojištění)

- **Employed: no minimum advances.** Your employer already pays health insurance on your salary [V] ([Vyfakturuj](https://www.vyfakturuj.cz/fakturopedie/osvc-jako-vedlejsi-cinnost/), [jakpodnikat.cz](https://www.jakpodnikat.cz/zdravotni-pojisteni.php), [VZP 2026](https://www.vzp.cz/o-nas/tiskove-centrum/otazky-tydne/platby-zdravotniho-pojisteni-v-roce-2026)).
- There is **no threshold** for health insurance. Any profit is charged **13.5% of 50% of profit = 6.75%** [B rate/base]. You pay it in one sum after filing the annual statement (Přehled) with your insurer. In later years, advances are calculated from the previous year's actual profit. "No minimum" does not mean "no advances" [V/B].

### 2.3 Income tax (daň z příjmů fyzických osob)

- Business income is taxed under **§ 7 ZDP** and added to your salary (§ 6) in **one personal tax return (daňové přiznání)**. **15%** applies up to **1,762,812 CZK** of total tax base in 2026, and **23%** above that [V] ([Podnikatel.cz](https://www.podnikatel.cz/clanky/od-jake-vyse-mzdy-a-prijmu-se-bude-v-roce-2026-platit-23-sazba-dane-z-prijmu/), [e15](https://www.e15.cz/finexpert/danove-priznani/dan-23-procent-az-od-vyssiho-prijmu-kdo-ji-v-roce-2026-zaplati-1429566)). The taxpayer credit of 30,840 CZK is normally already used against your salary, so **side profit is taxed at a marginal 15%** [V credit amount].
- **Expenses:** either actual expenses kept in tax records (daňová evidence), or a **flat-rate percentage of revenue (výdajový paušál)** [V] ([Fakturoid](https://www.fakturoid.cz/almanach/dane/pausalni-vydaje), [Vyfakturuj](https://www.vyfakturuj.cz/fakturopedie/pausalni-vydaje/), [BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/osvc-stale-vyuzivaji-pausalni-vydaje-komu-se-pri-vypoctu-dane-i-po-zmenach-vyplati/)):
  - **80%** for crafts (řemeslné živnosti) and agriculture: max **1,600,000 CZK** of expenses (reached at 2M CZK revenue)
  - **60%** for other živnosti (volná, vázaná, koncesovaná): max **1,200,000 CZK**
  - **40%** for other self-employment without a trade licence (e.g. freelance professions, copyright income): max **800,000 CZK**
  - **30%** for rental: max 600,000 CZK
  - Unchanged for 2026 [V]. Side effect of using a paušál: if the paušál-based business profit exceeds 50% of your total tax base, you lose the spouse credit and child tax benefit [B]. This rarely matters when you have a salary.
- **Selling mostly digital products under a volná živnost?** The **60% paušál** is usually far better than actual expenses, because real costs are low (see example B below).
- **Your own social and health insurance payments are not tax-deductible** [B]. A business **loss cannot be set off against salary income**; it can only be carried forward for up to 5 years [B].
- **Flat tax regime (paušální daň): generally NOT available to you.** A condition of entry is having no employment income, except income taxed by final withholding (for example a small DPP) [V] ([FS FAQ on paušální daň](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/pausalni-dan/dotazy-a-odpovedi/dotazy-a-odpovedi-k-pausalni-dani), [Podnikatel.cz manual 2026](https://www.podnikatel.cz/clanky/velky-manual-k-pausalni-dani-osvc-pro-rok-2026/)).
- **Tax return:** required every year you have § 7 income. Your employer's annual settlement (roční zúčtování) cannot cover it. For the 2025 tax year the deadlines were 1 Apr 2026 on paper, 4 May 2026 electronically, and 1 Jul 2026 through an advisor [V] ([Finanční správa](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/financni-sprava/media-a-verejnost/tiskove-zpravy-gfr/tiskove-zpravy-2026/dan-z-prijmu-za-rok-2025-tyden-do-konce-lhuty), [Investice.cz](https://www.investice.cz/clanek/danove-priznani-2026-papirove-do-1-dubna-elektronicky-az-do-kvetna)). For **2026 income**: **1 Apr 2027**, or **3 May 2027** electronically (computed; 1 May 2027 is a Saturday). Ask your employer for the annual salary certificate (Potvrzení o zdanitelných příjmech) in February.
- **Records:** with a paušál you only need a record of revenue and receivables. With actual expenses you need full tax records (daňová evidence) [B].

### 2.4 Worked examples: profit from a volná živnost as a side activity, full year 2026

Assumptions: rate **24.5 CZK/EUR** (an estimate close to the 2025–26 range; the rate was not verified in this session). The full-year threshold of 117,521 CZK applies. Your salary keeps the total tax base under 1,762,812 CZK, and your salary has already used the taxpayer credit. Profit here means revenue minus actual expenses. Rounding follows the usual rules: tax base rounded down to hundreds, insurance rounded up.

**Example A: actual-expense accounting (e.g. physical products with real material costs)**

| | €5,000 profit | €20,000 profit |
|---|---|---|
| Profit in CZK | 122,500 | 490,000 |
| Income tax 15% | 18,375 | 73,500 |
| Social 29.2% × 55% (threshold 117,521 exceeded) | 19,674 | 78,694 |
| Health 13.5% × 50% | 8,269 | 33,075 |
| **Total levies** | **46,318 CZK (37.8%)** | **185,269 CZK (37.8%)** |
| **Net** | **76,182 CZK ≈ €3,110** | **304,731 CZK ≈ €12,440** |
| Monthly advances the following year (estimate, from this profit) | social ≈ 1,640, health ≈ 690 | social ≈ 6,560, health ≈ 2,760 |

Note that €5,000 at 24.5 CZK/EUR is just **above** the 117,521 CZK threshold. With profit of **€4,790 or less (≈117,500 CZK)**, social insurance is **zero** and total levies fall to about **21.75%** (15% + 6.75%). That is why the cliff in 2.1 matters.

**Example B: same revenue, 60% flat-rate expenses (good for digital products with low real costs)**

| | €5,000 **revenue** | €20,000 **revenue** |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue in CZK | 122,500 | 490,000 |
| Deemed profit (40% of revenue) | 49,000 | 196,000 |
| Income tax 15% | 7,350 | 29,400 |
| Social (only if over 117,521) | 0 | 31,478 |
| Health 6.75% | 3,308 | 13,230 |
| **Total levies** | **10,658 CZK (8.7% of revenue)** | **74,108 CZK (15.1% of revenue)** |

Rule of thumb: set aside **about 22%** of side profit while you stay under the social threshold, and **about 38%** above it (actual expenses). With the 60% paušál, set aside **9–15% of revenue** at these volumes.

## 3. VAT (DPH)

### 3.1 Czech VAT registration (plátce DPH)

- **2,000,000 CZK** turnover in a **calendar year**: you become a VAT payer from **1 January of the following year**. Alternatively, you can opt to register immediately [V].
- **2,536,500 CZK**: you become a VAT payer **from the day after** exceeding it. The application is due within **10 working days** [V] ([Finanční správa: changes from 1 Jan 2025](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/financni-sprava/novinky/novinky-2025/informace-ke-zmenam-v-platcovstvi-dph-2025), [Shoptet blog](https://blog.shoptet.cz/registrace-dph-zmeny/), [Pohoda](https://portal.pohoda.cz/dane-ucetnictvi-mzdy/dph/dph-v-roce-2025-1-dil-registrace-platce/)). These rules have applied since 2025 and are unchanged for 2026.
- Turnover (obrat) means **revenue**, not profit. At the scale in the worked examples you stay far below the threshold, so as a **non-payer (neplátce)** you **sell without Czech VAT**. That is a price advantage on the CZ market.
- Voluntary registration only pays off if your customers are VAT-registered businesses or you have large input VAT. That rarely applies to B2C products.

### 3.2 EU B2C distance sales and OSS (€10,000)

- If you sell physical goods shipped from CZ to consumers in other EU countries, **plus** B2C e-services and digital downloads sold outside marketplaces, and the total stays **below €10,000/yr across all other EU countries combined**, the sale is taxed as if in CZ. As a non-payer you charge **no VAT** [V] ([Shoptet](https://blog.shoptet.cz/dph-a-prodej-v-ramci-eu/), [jakpodnikat.cz](https://www.jakpodnikat.cz/zasilani-zbozi-eu-dph.php), [BusinessInfo OSS](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/jak-si-ulehcit-odvod-dph-pri-prodeji-do-zahranici-pomuze-vam-rezim-oss/)).
- **Above €10,000**, from the sale that crosses the threshold, you charge the **customer's country's VAT** (e.g. DE 19%, PL 23%, HU 27%). As a Czech non-payer you first register as **identified person (identifikovaná osoba)** or VAT payer, then join **OSS (One-Stop-Shop)** via Moje daně. OSS means one quarterly return in CZ for all EU countries, instead of registering in each country [V].
- **Pricing trap:** your prices must absorb foreign VAT once you pass €10,000. Plan margins for that from the start.

### 3.3 Identified person: the trap for Czech non-payers

- **What triggers it:** as a business (an OSVČ is a "taxable person" even when not VAT-registered), you **buy a service from a provider established in another country**, and the place of supply is CZ. Typical triggers: **Meta and Google ads, Pinterest or TikTok ads, Etsy listing and transaction fees (Etsy Ireland), Shopify subscriptions**, and foreign SaaS such as Canva or Adobe when bought for the business [V] ([BusinessInfo](https://www.businessinfo.cz/navody/identifikovana-osoba-a-platby-dph-povinnosti-pro-reklamy-google-adsense-preshranicni-sluzby-uber-aj/), [jakpodnikat.cz](https://www.jakpodnikat.cz/identifikovana-osoba-k-dph.php), [Shoptet blog](https://blog.shoptet.cz/e-shop-jako-identifikovana-osoba/)). **Etsy:** you become an identified person at the first fee paid, which is usually the first listing fee [V] ([Handmade byznys](https://www.handmadebyznys.cz/osoba-identifikovana-k-dph-a-etsy/)).
- **What you must do** [V]:
  1. **Register within 15 days** of the first such purchase (electronic form). You receive a DIČ (tax ID) of the form CZ + birth number.
  2. Enter the DIČ in your Meta, Google and Etsy billing settings, so that they invoice **without** VAT under the reverse charge.
  3. For **each month** in which you received such a service, file a **VAT return by the 25th of the following month** and pay **21% Czech VAT** on the fee. You have **no right to deduct** it.
  4. File an **EC Sales List (souhrnné hlášení)** only if **you** supply services to businesses in other EU countries.
  - Example: 3,000 CZK/month of Meta ads means 630 CZK of VAT plus one return each month (computed).
- **You remain a non-payer domestically.** You still sell to Czech customers without VAT [V].
- **Payment processing fees** (Stripe, PayPal, Etsy Payments) are generally VAT-exempt financial services. Some Czech guides nevertheless list them as triggers [V: this appears in the identified-person search results above], so confirm with your advisor [B].
- **Buying goods (not services) from EU suppliers:** as a non-payer you only become an identified person once your intra-EU goods purchases exceed **326,000 CZK/yr**. **However, if you give an EU supplier your DIČ, they invoice without VAT and you must self-assess Czech VAT.** Use the DIČ only for services [B].
- **Digital downloads sold on Etsy (possible extra obligation):** because Etsy is the deemed supplier to the consumer (3.5), your own supply is legally a B2B service to Etsy Ireland. That can make you an identified person for supplied services, with an EC Sales List to file. Czech advisors differ on this, so raise it in your one-hour consultation [B].
- **Penalties:** fines apply for late registration and late returns. Search results cite figures of up to 500,000 CZK for not registering [V: mention in search extract; B: exact law]. In practice the risk is modest amounts plus penalties, but the audit trail (Meta/Google invoices) is complete.

### 3.4 Margin scheme for second-hand goods (zvláštní režim pro použité zboží, § 90 ZDPH)

- **Only for VAT payers**, and voluntary. VAT is charged only on the **margin** (sale price minus purchase price), not the full price. Example: buy for 100,000 and sell for 120,000; VAT is 3,471 CZK on the 20,000 margin [V] ([Pohoda](https://portal.pohoda.cz/dane-ucetnictvi-mzdy/dph/zvlastni-rezim-dph-pro-obchodniky-s-pouzitym-zbozim/), [Money](https://money.cz/navod/postup-pri-vyuziti-zvlastniho-rezimu-dph-podle-%C2%A7-90-pri-prodeji-pouziteho-zbozi/)). No input deduction applies on those purchases [V].
- **Relevance:** irrelevant while you are a non-payer. It becomes important if a refurb or resale business grows past 2M CZK. Bonus: margin-scheme goods are **excluded from the EU distance-sales rules**, so VAT stays Czech even when shipped B2C across the EU, with no OSS needed [B].

### 3.5 Digital products to EU consumers

- The **place of supply is the customer's country**. Below the €10,000 combined EU threshold (3.2) a Czech non-payer charges no VAT. Above it you must charge the customer's VAT via OSS after registering as an identified person [V/B].
- **Etsy acts as deemed supplier for digital downloads:** Etsy **collects and remits EU VAT on all digital items delivered by automatic download** to EU buyers, whatever the seller's location [V] ([Etsy seller handbook: VAT on digital items](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/26557870596), [Etsy: EU digital rules](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/22848011323)). Etsy also collects **US sales tax** as a marketplace facilitator, and UK/Norway/Australia-style VAT and GST on the relevant sales [B].
- **Etsy is NOT the deemed supplier for physical goods you ship from CZ to EU buyers.** You handle those under 3.2 [B] ([Hellotax overview](https://hellotax.com/blog/vat-on-digital-platforms/)).
- **Own website:** use a **merchant of record** (e.g. Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, and Gumroad in its current model) that sells in its own name and handles worldwide VAT and GST. This avoids OSS and non-EU digital VAT registrations. Cost is about 5% plus a fixed fee per sale (estimate from typical published pricing; verify) [B].

## 4. Product rules (physical goods)

### 4.1 GPSR: General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988, applies since 13 Dec 2024 [B]

This applies to all consumer products not covered by more specific EU safety law, including handmade and 3D-printed items. As a **Czech maker** you are the **manufacturer**. For your own products you do **not** need a separate EU responsible person, because you are EU-established.

1. **Internal risk analysis and technical documentation** for each product type: materials, intended and foreseeable use, choking/sharp/flammability/chemical hazards, and relevant standards. Keep it for **10 years**. For simple products one page per type is enough.
2. **Product identification:** type, batch or serial number **on the product**, or on the packaging or accompanying document if the product is too small.
3. **Manufacturer name, postal address and electronic contact** (email or web) on the product, packaging or accompanying document.
4. **Instructions and safety warnings** in the **language of the consumer's country** (Czech for CZ buyers, German for DE, and so on).
5. **Online listings** must show: manufacturer name and postal/electronic address; the **EU responsible person** if the maker is outside the EU (this matters if you **resell** non-EU-made goods; you may then be the importer yourself); a picture and product identifiers; and warnings and safety information. **Etsy, Amazon and eBay now have mandatory GPSR fields** for this.
6. **Complaints and accidents:** keep a register of complaints and recalls, take corrective action, and report dangerous products and accidents via the EU **Safety Business Gateway**. The Czech enforcement authority is the Czech Trade Inspection Authority (**ČOI**).
- **Resellers (distributors)** have lighter duties: check that the labeling, manufacturer information and instructions are present.

### 4.2 Packaging EPR: Czech Republic (EKO-KOM) [B]

- Law: Packaging Act (zákon č. 477/2001 Sb., o obalech). An e-shop that packs goods into boxes or envelopes and sends them to Czech consumers **places packaging on the market**.
- **Small-quantity relief:** businesses that place **under about 300 kg of packaging per year** (roughly under 4.5M CZK turnover) are exempt from the take-back and recovery obligation, but must still keep **records (evidence)** of the packaging they put on the market. A small side e-shop is almost certainly under this, because 300 kg is about 3,000 shipping boxes of 100 g (estimate). Confirm the exact current wording at mzp.cz or ekokom.cz. If you exceed it, join **EKO-KOM**, which offers a simplified small-client contract.
- **EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, (EU) 2025/40)** applies generally from **12 Aug 2026**. It harmonises EPR and labeling and phases in further duties from 2028 to 2030. It adds an obligation to appoint an **authorised representative** in each member state where you sell packaged goods B2C at a distance. Watch for the Czech implementing law.

### 4.3 Germany (VerpackG/LUCID) and other countries [B]

- **Germany (VerpackG):** **no minimum quantity.** Before the first B2C shipment to a German consumer you must (1) register free of charge in **LUCID** (Zentrale Stelle Verpackungsregister) and (2) **license** your shipping and sales packaging with a **dual system** (e.g. Lizenzero, Interzero, Grüner Punkt). **Estimate:** about **€30–100/year** for a few hundred kilograms, based on the published small-volume price levels these online licensors used in 2024–25; verify. **Marketplaces (Amazon, eBay, Etsy) must block unregistered sellers** and ask for your LUCID number. Report volumes annually in LUCID and to the system.
- **France:** EPR under the AGEC law. You need a **UIN (Unique Identification Number)** for packaging (CITEO), and separately for categories such as textiles, furniture, toys, sports goods and DIY. Marketplaces must collect the UIN or pay the eco-fee on your behalf. Non-French sellers often need an **authorised representative**, so the cost can be significant. Test France via a marketplace that handles EPR, or skip it at first.
- **Austria** (authorised representative for foreign distance sellers), **Spain** (producer registry since 2025) and **Netherlands, Belgium, Italy** have their own packaging EPR registrations. **Plan: CZ + DE + SK first**, and add other countries only when volume justifies it.

### 4.4 WEEE and batteries (only if you sell electronics) [B]

- **WEEE:** in CZ, Act 542/2020 Sb. on end-of-life products requires registration in the Czech producers' register, usually via a collective scheme (e.g. REMA, ASEKOL, ELEKTROWIN), plus take-back information for customers. **For each other EU country you ship to consumers, you need WEEE registration there**, via an authorised representative. In Germany that means stiftung ear, which costs hundreds of euros a year (estimate).
- **Batteries:** the EU **Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542** has required EPR registration since Aug 2025, including for batteries **built into devices**. Distance sellers need an authorised representative in each member state. Further labeling duties phase in from 2026–2027.
- **Practical advice:** avoid mains-powered or battery-containing products at the start. The per-country WEEE and battery overhead kills small margins. If you do sell electronics, start **CZ-only** or sell via marketplaces with fulfilment partners who handle EPR.

### 4.5 CE marking basics [B]

- CE marking applies **only** to products covered by specific EU "harmonised" laws. For small sellers the main ones are **toys** (Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC, replaced by the new Toy Safety Regulation with a transition to about 2030), **electrical equipment** (LVD, EMC, RoHS, RED for radio devices), **PPE**, **machinery** and **construction products**.
- The maker runs a conformity assessment, usually self-assessment against standards such as EN 71-1/2/3 for toys, keeps technical documentation, signs an **EU Declaration of Conformity**, and affixes CE.
- **Jewelry, candles, décor and most 3D-printed household items are NOT CE-marked.** They fall under GPSR plus chemical rules. **A 3D-printed item marketed for children under 14 is a toy**, which brings CE, EN 71 testing and small-parts rules. Avoid this at the start.
- **Jewelry chemical limits** (REACH Annex XVII): nickel release (0.5 µg/cm²/week for skin contact; 0.2 for piercing posts), lead (<0.05% by weight) and cadmium limits. Ask suppliers for test certificates.

### 4.6 CLP: candles, wax melts, diffusers, cleaning products [B]

- **Fragrance oils** are usually classified as hazardous: skin sensitiser, aquatic toxicity. Depending on fragrance load, a finished scented candle or **wax melt** may need a **CLP label**: hazard pictogram, signal word, H and P statements, the supplier's name, address and phone, and nominal quantity. It must be in the **language of the country of sale**.
- Your **fragrance supplier's SDS or IFRA/CLP calculator** tells you what applies at your fragrance percentage.
- Some classified mixtures also need a **UFI code and poison-centre (PCN) notification**.
- Candle safety pictograms follow **EN 15494**.
- The **revised CLP (Reg. (EU) 2024/2865)** adds label formatting rules (minimum font size and so on) phasing in 2026–2027. Check current dates, because an EU "omnibus" simplification may have shifted them.

### 4.7 Cosmetics and food: the burden in one line each [B]

- **Cosmetics:** vázaná živnost + Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009: a **Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR)** by a qualified assessor (estimate: €150–500 per formula, from typical EU assessor pricing), GMP, a product information file, **CPNP notification** before sale, and INCI labeling. This is heavy for a side business.
- **Food:** food-business registration with **SZPI** (Czech Agriculture and Food Inspection Authority) for plant-based foods and online food sales, or with **KVS** (regional veterinary administration) for food of animal origin. Also HACCP, hygiene premises requirements and food-information labeling (Reg. 1169/2011) with allergens. Some food types need a řemeslná živnost.

### 4.8 Jewelry hallmarking (puncovnictví) in CZ [B]

- **Hallmarking Act (puncovní zákon č. 539/1992 Sb.):** articles of **gold, silver, platinum and palladium** sold in CZ must be **assayed and hallmarked by the Czech Assay Office (Puncovní úřad)** before sale. Very light articles below the weight limits in the Act are exempt; my recollection is about 0.5 g for gold and 1 g for silver, but verify at puncovniurad.cz.
- You also need to **notify the Assay Office** before you start trading in precious-metal articles (oznamovací povinnost), and register a **maker's/responsibility mark** if you are the maker.
- Fees are per item and modest (estimate: tens of CZK per piece). The item must be delivered to an office such as Prague, Jablonec nad Nisou or Brno, which adds days of lead time.
- **Other markets:** Germany has **voluntary** hallmarking. France, Ireland, the Netherlands and the **UK** (silver ≥7.78 g) have **mandatory** hallmarking. The Czech hallmark is recognised in other Vienna Convention states only if it is the CCM (Common Control Mark).
- **Practical route:** buy findings and chains that are already hallmarked, or have a Turnov/Jablonec workshop that is already registered do the casting and hallmarking.

## 5. Selling channels

### 5.1 DAC7 platform reporting [V]

EU Directive 2021/514, in force since 2023. Platforms (Etsy, eBay, Amazon, Vinted, Aukro, Airbnb and others) report sellers with **30 or more sales OR €2,000 or more** in a calendar year to the tax authority. The report includes name, address, tax ID, IBAN, turnover, fees and number of sales. The platform asks you for your tax ID in advance ([Finanční správa DAC7](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/mezinarodni-spoluprace/mezinarodni-zdanovani-prime-dane/dac7), [Vinted DAC7](https://www.vinted.cz/dac7), [iROZHLAS](https://www.irozhlas.cz/ekonomika/dane-evropska-unie-vinted-aukro-financni-urady_2211080635_har)). **Being reported does not by itself create a tax liability**, but business sellers will be matched against their tax returns.

### 5.2 Platform rules: business vs. private sellers [B]

| Platform | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Etsy** | Handmade, vintage (20+ years old) and craft supplies only. You must disclose **production partners** (e.g. a casting workshop or print shop). EU sellers must give business details: IČO and address as the GPSR/DSA "trader" information, which is shown to buyers. Fees are roughly a $0.20 listing fee + 6.5% transaction fee + payment processing (about 4% + a fixed fee, varying by country) + optional Offsite Ads at 12–15% (from Etsy's fee pages; verify current values). |
| **eBay (ebay.de etc.)** | Register a **business account** when selling commercially. That requires legal notice/imprint (Impressum), withdrawal right, T&Cs, GPSR fields and the LUCID number for DE. A private account is only for your own used items. |
| **Amazon** | Professional seller account (about €39/month + referral fees of about 7–15%; estimate from Amazon.de fee pages; verify). KYC with business documents. Mandatory EPR numbers for DE (LUCID) and FR (UIN) in the relevant categories. |
| **Vinted** | Standard accounts are **for private individuals selling their own items only**. Businesses must use **Vinted Pro**, where available in your country (check CZ availability). Running a hidden business on a private account breaches the terms and EU unfair-practices law. |
| **CZ marketplaces** | Aukro, Allegro.cz, Kaufland.cz, Heureka Marketplace and Fler.cz (Czech handmade) all expect IČO-registered traders for recurring sales. |

### 5.3 Consumer law essentials (Civil Code, Act 89/2012 Sb.; Consumer Protection Act 634/1992 Sb.) [B]

- **14-day right of withdrawal** for distance sales, with no reason required. If you fail to inform the buyer properly, it extends by **12 months**. You refund the price **plus the cheapest standard delivery cost** within 14 days. The buyer pays the return shipping **if you told them so in advance**.
  - **Exceptions:** goods **made to the customer's specifications or clearly personalised** (engraved names, custom sizes), sealed hygiene items that have been unsealed, and **digital content** once download has begun with the buyer's express consent and acknowledgement that they lose the withdrawal right. Build that checkbox into your checkout.
- **New from 19 June 2026:** EU Directive (EU) 2023/2673 requires online traders to provide a clearly labelled **"withdraw from contract" function (button)** on the interface. Check the Czech transposition status and whether your e-shop platform (Shoptet, Shopify) has implemented it.
- **Warranty for defects (práva z vadného plnění): 24 months.** For **used goods** this can be shortened to **12 months** if agreed, so put it in the T&Cs and on the listing. Defects appearing **within 1 year** are presumed to have existed at delivery. Handle complaints (reklamace) within **30 days**.
- **Price rules:** prices must include all taxes. A discount must be announced against the **lowest price in the previous 30 days**. Disclose whether reviews are verified.
- **Business terms (obchodní podmínky) essentials:** your full name, IČO, business address, email and phone, and the trade-licence registration; the product, total price and delivery costs; payment and delivery terms; withdrawal information and a **model withdrawal form**; the complaints procedure (reklamační řád); the **used-goods 12-month clause** if relevant; the out-of-court dispute body for CZ consumers (**ČOI**, adr.coi.cz); a GDPR privacy notice (separate document); and cookie consent.
  - The EU **ODR platform link is no longer required**: the platform was shut down in July 2025.
  - The **European Accessibility Act** (from 28 Jun 2025) exempts **microenterprises** (fewer than 10 staff and ≤€2M turnover) providing services.
- **Czech-language labeling and instructions** are required for goods sold to Czech consumers (Consumer Protection Act § 11).
- **Watch list:** the post-2025 government has announced plans to reintroduce **electronic sales registration (EET)**. Historically it did not cover bank transfers and online card payments. Check the status before any in-person sales.

### 5.4 Shipping from CZ (Packeta / Zásilkovna) [B, prices are estimates]

- **Coverage:** Packeta runs its **own pickup-point and Z-BOX network in CZ, SK, HU, RO and PL**. It delivers to **most EU countries** (DE, AT and others) through partner carriers for home delivery or partner pickup points. It also offers some non-EU destinations. The e-shop plugin integrates with Shoptet, WooCommerce and Shopify.
- **Rough price levels, estimate** (recalled from Packeta's 2024–25 e-shop price list, excluding VAT, parcel under about 2 kg; verify at packeta.com):

  | Destination | Estimated price (CZK) |
  |---|---|
  | CZ pickup point or Z-BOX | 65–90 |
  | CZ home delivery | 90–130 |
  | SK, PL, HU, RO pickup point | 80–160 |
  | DE, AT home delivery | 150–300 |
  | Other EU countries | 200–450 |

  Budget **€4–12 per EU parcel** in unit economics until you have a quote.
- **Letters and small flat items** (earrings, digital-accessory items) often go cheaper as a **Česká pošta letter**, but with no or limited tracking. That is a returns and "not received" risk on marketplaces.

### 5.5 Exporting to the USA after the end of de minimis [B, volatile: verify with CBP and your carrier before shipping]

- **Since 29 Aug 2025** the US **suspended duty-free "de minimis" treatment (goods up to $800) for all countries**. **Every parcel, including a €20 pair of earrings, is dutiable** and needs full customs data: HS code, country of origin, value. A statutory repeal of de minimis was also legislated in 2025, effective **1 July 2027**.
- **EU goods:** under the Aug 2025 EU–US framework, most EU goods faced a **15% all-in tariff ceiling**. In **early 2026 the US Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs**. The administration moved to other legal bases, including a temporary global tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act. **The exact rate that applies to an EU parcel in Oct 2026 must be checked at the time of shipping.** Assume **10–15% ad valorem** plus possible category-specific tariffs (steel/aluminium content, etc.).
- **Practical impact on small sellers:**
  - Several EU posts, **including Česká pošta**, suspended or restricted US goods shipments in late Aug 2025 and later reopened with changed procedures. Check current Česká pošta US status.
  - Carriers charge the recipient **brokerage or processing fees**, often more than the duty on low-value items. That leads to refused parcels and bad reviews.
  - Prefer **DDP (delivered duty paid)** labels via DHL Express, UPS, FedEx or a cross-border broker, and build duty into the price.
  - **Etsy does not collect US import duty** on physical items.
  - **Digital products are unaffected**: no customs apply.
- **Conclusion:** the US is fine for **digital products**. For physical goods, treat it as **secondary** unless the item is high-value (over €80) and you ship DDP.

### 5.6 UK after Brexit (small parcels) [B]

- Goods to UK consumers in consignments of **£135 or less**: **UK VAT (20%) is charged at the point of sale**, not at the border. If you sell **directly** (own site), you must **register for UK VAT from the first sale**, because there is no threshold for non-established sellers. If you sell **via a marketplace (Etsy, eBay, Amazon), the marketplace is the deemed supplier** and collects it. **Selling to the UK via marketplaces is therefore the simple route.**
- **Over £135:** import VAT and duty at the border, paid by the buyer plus a courier fee, unless you ship DDP.
- The UK announced it will **end the £135 customs-duty relief for low-value imports (by 2029 at the latest)**, so watch for changes.
- The EU GPSR does not apply in Great Britain, but UK product safety law does. UK hallmarking is mandatory for silver over 7.78 g.

### 5.7 Sourcing materials from outside the EU [B]

- Since Jul 2021 **all imports pay import VAT** (no €22 exemption). The **€150 customs-duty exemption for low-value parcels is being abolished**: the Council agreed in 2025 to an interim **flat €3 duty per item** from **1 Jul 2026**, pending the EU customs data hub.
- As a non-payer you cannot reclaim import VAT, so price it in.

## 6. One-page checklist: go legal in the first 2 weeks

**Days 1–2: decide and prepare**

- [ ] Check your employment contract for a non-compete or a side-business clause. You need written consent only if you compete with your employer (§ 304 Labour Code).
- [ ] **Timing:** if Q4 2026 profit will be small, consider starting on **1 Jan 2027**. Registering in Oct 2026 lowers the 2026 social threshold to about 29,375 CZK.
- [ ] Choose the volná živnost fields: Velkoobchod a maloobchod, plus Výroba plastových a pryžových výrobků, the software/publishing fields, etc. Avoid řemeslná or vázaná products (precious-metal jewelry, cosmetics, food, mains electrics) until proven.
- [ ] Decide your expense method: the **60% paušál** is good for digital or low-cost goods; **actual expenses** suit material-heavy goods. You can switch year by year [B].

**Days 2–3: register (one form)**

- [ ] File the **JRF online at rzp.gov.cz** with bank ID: **800 CZK**. In the same form, tick registration with the **tax office**, **ČSSZ** (start of side SVČ) and your **health insurer**.
- [ ] Within about 5 working days: the IČO is issued and the business **data box** appears. Set up email notifications for it.

**Days 4–7: money and VAT**

- [ ] Open a **separate bank account** for the business. It is not mandatory for non-payers but makes records and DAC7 reconciliation easy. Order a free or cheap business card.
- [ ] Before the **first ad spend or first Etsy listing**: register as an **identified person (identifikovaná osoba)** via Moje daně (free, within 15 days of the first foreign service). Enter the DIČ in Meta, Google and Etsy billing. Put a monthly reminder for the **25th**: VAT return + payment of 21% on the previous month's foreign fees.
- [ ] Set up invoicing and records: Fakturoid, iDoklad, Vyfakturuj or a spreadsheet. Record every sale, including the marketplace payout reports.
- [ ] Set aside **22–38% of profit** (see 2.4) in a savings sub-account.

**Days 7–12: products and shop**

- [ ] Write a **GPSR risk analysis** (1 page per product type). Prepare labels with batch/ID, your name and address, email, and warnings in CS/DE/EN. Fill the GPSR fields on each marketplace.
- [ ] Before any sale to German consumers: register in **LUCID** (free) and buy a small **dual-system licence**. Keep a packaging weight log for CZ (EKO-KOM records).
- [ ] Candles or chemicals: CLP label from the supplier SDS. Jewelry: nickel/lead certificates from suppliers; hallmarking plan for silver or gold.
- [ ] Write the **T&Cs, returns and complaints policy, withdrawal form, and GDPR notice**: 14 days, 24 months (12 for used goods), ČOI as ADR, digital-content consent checkbox, and the withdrawal button if you run your own site.

**Days 12–14: go live**

- [ ] Configure shipping: Packeta account and plugin, a price table per country, and a DDP option or a "no US physical shipping" rule.
- [ ] Track the **€10,000 EU B2C** running total and the **2,000,000 CZK** CZ turnover in a monthly spreadsheet.
- [ ] Put the key dates in your calendar: the tax return (1 Apr / 3 May 2027), the ČSSZ and health **Přehledy** (one month after the return deadline), and the monthly identified-person VAT return (25th).
