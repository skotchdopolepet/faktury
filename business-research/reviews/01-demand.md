# Review 01, DEMAND lens: Vintage Czechoslovak glass, ceramics and Christmas-glass picker (C02)

Reviewer lens: demand and competition skeptic. Date: 2026-09-29.
Tags: **[verified: URL]** is a search-result snippet (from this review or from the files, as noted); **[files]** means the evidence is already in 02-main-loop-evidence.md or 06-verification.md; **[knowledge]** is background knowledge not checked this session; **[estimate]** is my own arithmetic, with the reasoning shown.
Web: one WebSearch was spent on glass *sold* prices. It returned only listing pages and WorthPoint entry titles, with no sold values visible (sources at the end).

## 1. Verdict

**WEAK, score 4/10.**

- **The dossier admits that demand, not supply, is the binding constraint.** By its own table it can source about 40 saleable pieces a month but expects to sell only 15–25 (§7: "Sourcing is not the binding constraint in year 1; sell-through is"). That is the reverse of what the brief asks for ("demand clearly bigger than supply"). What remains is a **price** asymmetry (buy low in CZ) on a **demand-constrained** shelf, which is a "list and hope" model with a cheap cost of goods.
- **Every glass and ceramics price in the dossier is an asking price.** The only *sold* comp in the file is for ornaments ($3.75 per piece). The unit economics use €55 (line A) and a €250 hammer (line E) with no realised-price evidence behind them.
- **The proof-of-model shops were built on US buyers,** about 90% of sales per Dwell [files], and that channel has been cut since Aug 2025: Etsy traffic for international sellers is down 20–50%, and one seller reported −70% [files: 06-verification]. The EU-first pivot is untested. EU buyers also **have their own local supply of the same Czech export glass**, sold by zero-fee private sellers and shipped domestically for less than half of what it costs to ship from CZ [knowledge].
- It is not a KILL. Real transactions have happened, the top end (named, cased and art glass) does sell, the €1,000 test is cheap and falsifiable, and stock keeps some resale value. But the realistic income is hobby-grade.

## 3. The demand-lens questions

**Sold or asking prices?** Asking prices, for glass and ceramics throughout. The dossier is honest about this in §3, but it then builds §6 and §10 on those asks. Before any money is spent, the founder can get sold comps for free: eBay.de's "Verkaufte Artikel" filter, eBay.com sold listings and Catawiki closed lots (the latter two need a login). The orchestrator's web search could not pull them.

**How many competitors, and do they cover the gap?** Many, on every channel:
- **Etsy:** dedicated market pages for "czech sklo union", "sklo union", "sklo union glass", "vintage czech glass", "zemek glass vase" and "vintage czechoslovakian ornaments" [files]. A page exists only when a term has enough listings, so there are dozens to hundreds of sellers, including other CZ, SK, PL, HU and UA pickers and Western dealers [estimate].
- **Established CZ incumbent:** VintageRetroEu, with years of reviews and Star-Seller-type ranking advantages that a new shop lacks [knowledge].
- **eBay.de, Kleinanzeigen and Vinted:** zero-fee private sellers in the destination countries.
- **UK specialists:** 20thcenturyglass.com and others.
- **The top end:** Pamono and 1stDibs dealers, plus Aukro and Prague Auctions collectors on the buy side.

The gap is **not uncovered**. It has been worked for 10+ years, and a newcomer enters a mature arbitrage, not a fresh one.

**Does the gap close within 6–12 months?** The structural East/West gap doesn't close that fast. Three forces are already squeezing *this* entrant's slice, though:
1. The **buy side** is inflating. Czech clickbait on vase values ran through 2026 (Tiscali, Extrafit, Nespěchej), and 06-verification notes that CZ sellers are "increasingly aware" of what their pieces are worth [files].
2. The **US exit** is impaired now and doesn't depend on competitors noticing [files].
3. **Disintermediation** is possible: Czech private sellers can reach EU buyers directly through cross-border second-hand platforms such as Vinted's international catalogue. Whether CZ is included is **uncertain** [knowledge]. Where it is, the picker's margin on common pieces goes to zero.

**Rising or fading?**
- **US channel:** sharply down since Aug 2025 [files].
- **Etsy:** GMS is falling, projected at ~$11.2B for 2025 against $12.5B for 2024 [low-quality source cited in hunt/digital-products.md; 10-K marketplace GMS for 2025 is $10.46B].
- **The category:** the Eastern-bloc mid-century craze peaked around 2015–2021 in US and Western decor media [knowledge]. There is a modest counter-tailwind from the "colored vintage glass" and "granny-chic" interior trend on Instagram and TikTok [knowledge, unquantified].
- **Net for this plan:** flat EU demand, a shrinking US channel and a shrinking main platform.

**Are new entrants succeeding?** There is **no evidence either way**. Every proof point is an incumbent whose history predates 2025. No shop that started in 2024–26 is cited as reaching 20+ glass sales a month. Etsy's ranking favours shops with history, and since Aug 2025 it shows international items to US buyers less often [files]. Absence of evidence counts against the claim here, because the brief requires *proven* demand.

## 4. Fatal flaws (from the demand lens)

1. **The model fails the brief's core criterion.** It is demand-constrained by its own admission, so it offers no "demand clearly bigger than supply". The asymmetry is on the buy price, which a thin sell side can erase.
2. **The demand proof is from a regime that no longer exists.** US-heavy incumbents are the evidence, and the US channel is down 20–70%. The EU channel that is supposed to replace it has local supply of the same goods from zero-fee private sellers, and a structural shipping disadvantage of about €6–8 per parcel [estimate: €12–13 from CZ against €5–7 domestic DE].

Neither flaw is certain to be fatal, because the €1,000 test would reveal both. That is why this is WEAK and not KILL.

## 5. Fixable issues, with fixes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Prices are asks | **Before buying stock**, spend two evenings collecting 90-day **sold** prices on eBay.de, eBay.com and Catawiki closed lots for about 30 target designs (Rosice, Libochovice, Hermanova Huť, Zemek Rhapsody, Ditmar Urbach, ornament lots). Buy only designs with an EU median sold price ≥ €40 **and** ≥ 5 sales in 90 days. Replace the €55 in §6 with the measured median. |
| Unknown whether EU buyers pay a landed price that includes €12–13 shipping from CZ | Compare Kleinanzeigen and eBay.de private asks for the same 30 designs. If the local private ask plus €6 shipping is below our item price plus €13, drop that design for DE and target the UK or NL instead. |
| Day-60 gate falls in Q4, which inflates gift and ornament demand | Keep the gate, but judge it **without ornaments** and on **realised average item price ≥ €38** (not "net ≥ €22", which a few Catawiki outliers can mask). Make a **March 2027 re-test** mandatory whatever December showed: ≥ 12 non-ornament sales in March. |
| The sell-through gate is too loose relative to the aging rule | Track sell-through **by line and channel**. With the 180-day liquidation rule, a 5%/month sell-through means ~74% of listed stock is liquidated at cost (0.95⁶ ≈ 0.74) [estimate]. Require ≥ 8%/month on lines A and C, not blended. |
| "Realistic" case is really a top-decile outcome | Plan on 12–18 sales a month; treat 25+ as the optimistic case. |
| Ornaments into DE | Market ornament bundles to the UK, NL and the US (duty-inclusive, bundles ≥ €60) rather than to DE. Cap ornament stock at €150. |
| Weak differentiation | The only defensible edge is top-end identification (lines B and E) plus access to vyklízení bulk lots. Shift sourcing time towards clearance-firm lots and away from flea-market browsing, where dealers arrive first. |

## 6. Corrected month-12 estimate (monthly profit before income tax and levies)

Assumptions [estimate]:
- **Listings and sell-through:** 250–350 active listings, with blended monthly sell-through of 3% (pessimistic), 5% (realistic) and 9% (optimistic). My view is that 3–5% is normal for mid-tier vintage decor at a young shop, and 8–10% needs sharp pricing on eBay.de.
- **Line A net** at a realised €40 item price + €13 shipping: fees incl. 21% VAT ≈ €9.4, buy €8, shipping €12, packaging €3, breakage 5% ≈ €2.65, giving **≈ €18** (the dossier has €31).
- **Blended net per unit:** 55% A at €18, 30% C/D at €12, 10% US at €20 and 5% B/E at €90, giving **≈ €20**.
- **Fixed costs:** as in the dossier.

| Scenario | Units/month | Net/unit | Fixed | **M12 profit/month** | Hours/month | **€/hour** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic | 8 (250 × 3%) | €16 | €60 | **≈ €70** | ~30 | **≈ €2** |
| Realistic | 15 (300 × 5%) | €20 | €75 | **≈ €225** | ~33 | **≈ €7** |
| Optimistic | 30 (350 × 9%) | €27 | €100 | **≈ €710** | ~50 | **≈ €14** |

The hours count sourcing trips (you can't source much less than one trip a week and keep a pipeline), processing, listing time for stock that never sells, packing and ~3 h/month of admin, taken from dossier §8 and adjusted for lower sell-through.

At the realistic level, the €10k EU distance-sales VAT threshold (dossier §11) is **not a year-1 or year-2 issue**. EU B2C sales would be about €6–7k/yr.

Compared with the dossier (€575 realistic, €15–20/h), my realistic figure is **≈ 60% lower**. The drivers are ask→sold price (−€8 to −€13 per unit) and volume (a top-decile assumption corrected to median-competent).

## 7. Would you tell a friend to do this?

**Only if** they would enjoy the Saturday flea-market hunt anyway, accept that the likely outcome is €150–300/month at single-digit €/hour, and do two things:
1. **First** collect the free eBay.de and Catawiki sold comps. If mid-tier EU medians come in below €35, don't start at all.
2. Treat the €1,000 test as a way to learn the sourcing loop for **higher-value lines** that share the trips (tested cameras and lenses, C07), not as a business in itself.

As a stand-alone answer to "demand clearly bigger than supply, with proven paying demand", **no**.
