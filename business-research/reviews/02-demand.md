# Review 02, DEMAND lens: Tested film cameras + adapted vintage lens kits (C07)

Reviewer lens: demand and competition skeptic. Date: 2026-09-29.
Tags: **[verified: URL]** is a search-result snippet (from this review or from the files, as noted); **[files]** means the evidence is already in 02-main-loop-evidence.md or 06-verification.md; **[knowledge]** is background knowledge not checked this session; **[estimate]** is my own arithmetic, with the reasoning shown.
Web: I spent one WebSearch, on whether 2026 used film-camera prices are cooling. It returned only seller guides and SEO price pages, all saying the market is "strong". I found no independent sold-price data either way (sources at the end).

## 1. Verdict

**WEAK, score 5/10.**

- **Demand is real and liquid.** This is the strongest paying-demand evidence among the dossiers: a market that is years old, runs on many channels and has visible transaction volume. A fairly priced tested camera or lens on eBay.de sells. That is why this is not a KILL, and why it scores above the glass picker (01-demand, 4/10).
- **The brief's core test fails at market level: demand is not clearly bigger than supply.**
  - The same bodies and lenses sit on eBay.de, Kleinanzeigen and Vinted in the thousands, many from **zero-fee German private sellers** [knowledge].
  - Japanese exporters ship volume, and EU refurbishers (Kamerastore, Film Camera Store, Dutch Thrift) already sell exactly the "tested, warranted" product.
  - "Shrinking working stock" is attrition over decades, not a shortage buyers feel today. The dossier cites no waitlists, sold-out signals, wanted-to-buy threads or dealers who can't keep stock.
  - The dossier half-admits this in its own line 1: "a small, labour-bound resale business rather than an asymmetric bet."
- **The margin rests on two spreads that nobody has measured.**
  - **The CZ buy price for untested lots.** Every figure in the "CZ buy" column of §4 is an [estimate]. The only verified CZ prices (Bazoš) show **zero or negative** spread [files].
  - **The tested/serviced premium over as-is.** All three scorers cite "~2×", but that rests on a single *asking* listing (a serviced Helios at $233, marked down from $333).
- **Every exit price is an asking price or a retailer's list price.** Once I correct them to realistic EU realised prices, net per unit falls 25–50%. My realistic month-12 figure is **≈ €300/month at ≈ €8/hour**, about half the dossier's €600 at €15–20/hour.

## 3. The demand-lens questions

**Sold prices or asking prices?** Asking prices, almost throughout. §3–4 hold roughly 20 price points:
- **eBay.de asks:** AE-1, Pancolar, Helios, Praktica.
- **Retailers' list prices:** Brooklyn Film Camera, Dutch Thrift.
- **Japanese store asks:** tsumcamera.
- **CZ Bazoš asks.**
- **One SEO "flip" average,** US-based.

**None of them is an EU sold price.** The dossier says so in its tag legend, and week 3 of its plan collects sold comps. But §6 and §10 are built on asks, and the sold-comp check comes *after* the licence, tools and stock orders are already under way.

**How many competitors, and do they cover the gap?** Many, at every tier, and together they cover the positioning the dossier proposes ("film-tested, sample scans, returns"):
- **The price anchor: zero-fee private sellers in the buyers' own countries.** eBay.de has charged private sellers no selling fees since 2023, and Kleinanzeigen and Vinted cost them nothing [knowledge]. Germany is also the home market for CZJ, Pentacon and Meyer glass, and the dossier admits GDR gear is "*more* plentiful in Germany" (§4). **Line C sells GDR lenses into the country that has more of them.**
- **Japanese exporters:** dozens of eBay stores grading "Exc+5 / Mint", dominant in hyped compacts and Japanese SLRs [verified store: dossier §5; knowledge].
- **EU refurbishers with warranties:**
  - Kamerastore (FI) has a large in-house workshop and graded stock, and also buys gear from consumers [knowledge, uncertain on buy-in reach].
  - Film Camera Store (UK) and Dutch Thrift (NL) [files].
  - Dozens of DE, AT and NL analog shops sell online [knowledge].
- **Ukrainian sellers** hold the Soviet lens + adapter kit niche [knowledge].
- **Substitutes for "character" lenses:** new manual primes from TTArtisan, 7Artisans and Meike at €60–150 in native mirrorless mounts, a new "bubble bokeh" 100 mm from TTArtisan, and Meyer-Optik Görlitz reissues of the Trioplan and Primoplan [knowledge]. These cap the price of the vintage originals. Chinese adapters at €10–15 also make the "kit" trivial for a buyer to assemble, which hunt/base-rates already flagged.
- **Czech flippers** already list "tested" Praktica and Zenit sets at ≈ €125 [files], and compete for the same untested lots.
- **Catawiki** weekly camera auctions and Kamerastore's buy-in are further competitors for premium finds and for sellers' stock [knowledge].

The gap is **covered**. The founder would enter a 10-year-old arbitrage as its newest, smallest and highest-cost seller in the main destination market.

**Does the gap close within 6–12 months?** For tested and hyped units it has **already closed**: Czech asks match or beat EU exit prices [files]. The untested-lot spread is contested now by three groups:
- Czech flippers with the same alerts.
- House-clearance firms that list directly on Bazoš and Aukro [knowledge].
- Film-camera flipping as a popular social-media side hustle [knowledge].

2026 brings one new tailwind: the €3/item duty plus import VAT make Japanese imports ~20% dearer. That favours **every** EU-located seller equally, including the German private sellers and refurbishers, so it gives the Czech founder no edge over them.

**Is the trend rising or fading?** Mixed, and flat overall:
- **Japanese mechanical SLRs:** flat to slightly rising (2025) [files]. This is the healthiest line.
- **Hyped compacts:** plateau and softening signals (the mju II "still near $250"). The dossier itself calls the $2k T2 "top of cycle".
- **Adapted vintage lenses:** the boom rode the 2015–2022 wave of mirrorless adoption [knowledge]. Cheap new AF and manual primes now compete, and hunt/base-rates flagged "price softening after the 2020–22 adapted-lens boom" without checking it. It is still unchecked, and this is the line the dossier rates as its best €/hour.
- **A substitute for line A's buyer:** Gen-Z's retro-camera budget is partly moving to 2000s CCD digicams, which have zero running cost. Their resale prices are up 2–3× [hunt/demand-signals.md], and PetaPixel ran "Gone are the days of universally cheap used digital cameras" in Aug 2026 [verified: see sources]. It is the same 18–35 buyer the dossier targets for compacts.
- **Net:** a stable niche with pockets of softness, not a rising wave.

**Are new entrants succeeding?** There is **no evidence either way**.
- The dossier cites no seller that started in 2024–26 and reached 15+ sales a month.
- The incumbents it names (Kamerastore, Brooklyn Film Camera, Dutch Thrift) scaled before 2023 or run physical shops [knowledge].
- The "30–50% success" figure in hunt/base-rates is a hunter's estimate, not data.
- Under the brief, absence of evidence counts against the claim.

## 4. Fatal flaws (from the demand lens)

1. **Liquidity without scarcity.** The market is deep, but it is deep on *both* sides. There is no supply gap that lets a newcomer set prices, and the realised exit price is set by fee-free private sellers in the buyers' own countries. This fails the brief's "demand clearly bigger than supply" criterion. What remains is a labour business with a thin, competed buy-side spread.
2. **The two numbers that make the margin are unmeasured:** the CZ untested-lot buy price and the tested-vs-as-is sold premium. If either comes in at half of what is assumed, net per unit falls to €15–25 and €/hour to single digits.

Neither is *certainly* fatal. Both can be tested for €0 in two to three weeks before any stock is bought, and stock can be liquidated at roughly cost. That makes this WEAK, not KILL.

**A specific downgrade: line C, the lens kits.** The dossier rates it the best line (€58 net, €39/hour). On the demand lens it is the weakest:
- It sells GDR glass into Germany, against zero-fee German private sellers.
- The kit premium is replicable with a €12 adapter.
- New Chinese manual lenses and Meyer reissues cap the price.
- The only realised-looking price in the file (Helios at ~$50–60) is half the asks.

Corrected, a CZJ/Meyer kit nets ≈ €30, about €20/hour. A Helios kit nets ≈ €8, about €7/hour, so drop it.

## 5. Fixable issues, with fixes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Exit prices are asks | Move the dossier's week-3 task ("20 eBay.de sold comps × 15 models") to **week 1, before the licence or any tools**, and make it a gate. Use eBay.de "Verkaufte Artikel" and Catawiki closed lots, over 90 days. Keep a model only if its **median sold price ≥ 3.3× the maximum buy price** and it has **≥ 8 sales in 90 days** on eBay.de. Replace every price in §6 with the measured medians. |
| The proof premium is assumed (~2×) | For 10 models, compare sold medians for "getestet / Filmtest / Probescans" against "ungetestet / ohne Gewähr / defekt". Keep a model only if the premium is ≥ 60% **and** ≥ €35 in absolute terms. If fewer than 5 models qualify, don't start. |
| The buy side is entirely estimated | For 2–3 weeks, log every CZ listing matching the dossier's alert terms ("pozůstalost fotoaparáty", "sbírka fotoaparátů", "objektiv M42", brand names) on Bazoš, Aukro (ended auctions) and Vinted CZ, with price and how fast it disappears. Count how many would qualify at ≤ 30% of the measured sold median. **If fewer than 10 a month qualify, don't start.** It costs €0 and ~6 hours. |
| German private sellers set the mid-tier price | For each target model, compare the cheapest DE private *sold* price + €5 shipping with our price + €7. Drop the model for DE if we are more than 15% dearer, and keep it only for CZ/SK. |
| The EEE lines' addressable market is smaller than implied | Lines A and B (battery-metered bodies) can reach only CZ/SK, plus DE once registered with stiftung ear. Every other EU country needs its own WEEE registration [quickstart §4.4]. Model months 1–5 as CZ/SK-only for those lines, with CZ realised prices (usually below DE), and model DE-only after that. Only line C and battery-free bodies (Flexaret, Zenit E) go EU-wide. |
| Lens line positioning | Stop competing on the "kit". Sell individually tested copies of higher-value glass (Flektogon 35/2.4 MC, Trioplan, Primoplan, Orestor), with test images at wide open. Drop the Helios, Jupiter and common Pancolar kits unless their measured sold medians clear the gate. |
| Hyped compacts | Buy only where the VAT edge over Japan applies (mju II, T-series, Big Mini). Sell them on eBay.de or Catawiki, and only at ≤ 45% of the measured sold median, given the plateau signals. |
| The kill criteria don't test price realisation | Add at day 60: **realised average price ≥ 85% of the plan price, per line.** A line can clear "net ≥ €30" through a single lucky find while its core SKUs undersell. |
| Trend risk is unmonitored | Each quarter, track eBay.de sold medians for 5 bellwethers: mju II, Trip 35, AE-1P + 50, Pancolar 50/1.8, Flektogon 35/2.4. If a line's bellwether falls > 15% over two quarters, cut that line's stock. |

## 6. Corrected month-12 estimate (monthly profit before income tax and levies)

**Per-unit net with realised prices.** I keep the dossier's §6 cost lines (fees at 10.4% + €0.54, ads at 3.63% of the total, shipping, packaging, seals and test roll, reserve and WEEE) and change only the price, plus the buy cost on line C. All figures are [estimate].

| Line | Dossier price → realised | Dossier net → corrected net | Hours | €/hour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: mid compact | €120 → **€100** | €45 → **€29** | 2.8 | ≈ €10 |
| B: SLR kit | €170 → **€150** | €58 → **€42** | 3.25 | ≈ €13 |
| C: CZJ/Meyer kit (buy €55 → €50) | €160 → **€120** | €58 → **€30** | 1.5 | ≈ €20 |
| C: Helios kit | €75 → **€55** | €24 → **€8** | 1.25 | ≈ €7 (drop) |

**Volume.** I cut the realistic volume from 16 to 12 units a month. The cause is not buyer demand: it is the contested buy side (fix 3). The realistic mix is 5 A, 3 B, 3 C and 1 Flexaret or premium find, which blends to ≈ €30–34 net per unit after extra friction from DE withdrawal-right returns. I use €30.

| Scenario | Units/month | Net/unit | Fixed | **M12 profit/month** | Hours/month | **€/hour** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic: sourcing-starved, realised prices at the low end | 7 | €22 | €60 | **≈ €95** | ~22 | **≈ €4** |
| Realistic | 12 | €30 | €60 | **≈ €300** | ~37 | **≈ €8** |
| Optimistic: house-clearance supplier, measured premium holds | 20 | €42 | €60 | **≈ €780** | ~52 | **≈ €15** |

**How the hours are counted.** For the realistic case: 5 × 2.8 + 3 × 3.25 + 3 × 1.5 + 1 × 2.8 ≈ 31 h of unit work, plus ~4 h of admin, plus ~2 h relisting stale stock and sorting duds, giving ≈ 37 h. The unit times are the dossier's own §8 figures.

At the realistic level, EU distance sales are ≈ €10–14k a year gross, so the €10k OSS threshold (dossier §11) **is** a year-1/year-2 issue and must be decided up front, as the dossier says. Profit (≈ 88k CZK/yr) stays under the 117,521 CZK social-insurance threshold.

My realistic figure is **≈ 50% below the dossier's** €600 at €15–20/hour. The drivers are asks → realised prices (−€16 to −€28 per unit) and a contested buy side (−25% volume).

## 7. Would you tell a friend to do this?

**Only if** three things are true:
1. They already shoot film or enjoy fixing mechanical things, so ~€8/hour works as a paid hobby.
2. They first run the three free checks: eBay.de sold medians, the tested-vs-untested sold premium, and a 2–3 week log of CZ buy-side listings. They start only if ≥ 5 models clear the gates and ≥ 10 qualifying lots a month appear.
3. They restrict the business to Japanese mechanical SLR kits, the better CZJ/Meyer glass as individual tested lenses, and opportunistic premium finds. They drop the Helios kits, the zoom compacts and anything bought at an informed Czech ask.

It also runs on the same sourcing trips as the glass picker (C02). Sharing trips lifts €/hour slightly, which is its best argument as a *line* in a Czech estate-picker shop rather than a stand-alone business.

As an answer to the brief's "demand clearly bigger than supply, with an edge from day one": **no**. Demand is proven. Scarcity and edge are not.
