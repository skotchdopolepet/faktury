**Dossier:** `dossiers/02-film-cameras-lenses.md` (C07: tested film cameras + adapted vintage lens kits)

## 1. Verdict

**WEAK: score 4/10.** It is legally executable and cheap to test, but the four-line plan is overloaded. It would deliver a paid hobby of roughly €350/month at about €8/hour, not an asymmetric bet.

- **The legal stack splits the business in two.** The lens line (C) is light: no WEEE and no electronics warranty. Lines A, B and D are almost all electrical equipment, because nearly every Japanese SLR on the SKU list has a battery meter or an electronic shutter. That means:
  - They cannot legally go to DE consumers until a German WEEE registration (stiftung ear, via an authorised representative) is in place.
  - Even then they can go **only** to DE. Every other EU country needs its own WEEE registration.
  - The dossier's "EU-shipped, VAT-free" positioning, its best edge, really applies only to lenses.
- **The dossier misses the AML duty.** A used-goods trader must identify the seller on *every* purchase. This collides with anonymous Bazoš, flea-market and house-clearance buying.
- **The 12-week plan cannot be run inside 10–15 h/week at beginner speed.**
  - It needs about 170–190 h. Weeks 2, 5, 10 and 11 need 20+ h each.
  - It starts in October, so the film-testing weeks land in November–January, when there is no weekday daylight to shoot test rolls.
  - Realistically it is a 16–20-week plan. Its day-60 gate would judge data that doesn't exist yet.
- **Sourcing is the binding constraint.**
  - Czech sellers are informed: the dossier's own table shows negative spreads on tested units.
  - The best SKUs are marked "thin" or "rare".
  - An employed founder misses daytime deals.
  - The one "scalable" channel, house-clearance firms, is untested.
- **Corrected realistic month-12 profit is about €340/month before tax, at about €8/h.** The dossier says €600/month at €15–20/h. Its own time model actually gives about €13/h.

## 4. Platform risk

1. **eBay.de, the single EU channel:**
   - **Fee-free private sellers.** eBay.de private sellers have paid no final-value fees since 2023 and can exclude warranty [knowledge]. Those are exactly the "as-is" competitors the proof premium is supposed to beat. The founder sells at ≈14% fees plus a 24-month warranty against them.
   - **New-seller limits and payout holds** [knowledge]. With zero feedback, the first 20–30 sales will be slow and cash-lagged.
   - **INAD exposure.** eBay's "item not as described" process favours buyers. Film cameras draw subjective claims ("light leak", "meter one stop off"). A few cases push a new account into "below standard", with a fee surcharge and lower visibility [knowledge]. Electronic compacts are the main INAD source.
   - **FX.** Check whether payouts to a CZ account are converted to CZK. Open a EUR account if needed [knowledge, verify].
2. **Etsy:** vintage eligibility is fine. It carries new-shop reserves and higher fees (~12–15%), and its US reach is impaired. It is at best a 1–2 sales/month channel for €150+ items [knowledge; file: 06-verification].
3. **Vinted CZ:** only via Vinted Pro, which is unverified for CZ (dossier §2). A private account used for business breaches the ToS, is an unfair commercial practice, and is visible via DAC7 at 30 sales [file: quickstart §5.1–5.2]. **Drop it unless Pro is available.**
4. **Bazoš/Aukro:** fine for a trader who discloses identity and offers withdrawal on shipped sales. Aukro fees apply [knowledge].
5. **Fee changes:** the Jul 2026 eBay.de fee change (5% for some used categories) is unverified for cameras (dossier §6). It could go either way.

## 5. The VAT ceiling (a structural cap on the "EU-shipped, VAT-free" edge)

- **When the ceiling bites.** At my realistic volume (see §11), 2027 brings about 125 units × €140 ≈ €17.5k revenue. About 55–65% of it goes outside CZ once DE WEEE is live, so **≈ €10–11k of EU B2C, crossing in about Nov–Dec 2027** [estimate]. The optimistic case crosses in mid-2027.
- **Without a fix,** DE VAT of 19% on the full price (≈ €22 per €140 unit) removes half the net margin.
- **The SME scheme keeps sales exempt.** It needs advance registration (up to 35 working days) for each target state, and quarterly reporting. Spain is excluded [verified above]. The DE condition (small-business thresholds: €25k prior year / €100k current year) is [knowledge, verify].
- **Execution rule:** track EU B2C revenue monthly, and **file for the SME scheme for DE (and AT if used) at about €6–7k cumulative**.

**Two further problems:**
1. **The calendar.** An October start puts weeks 5–12 in November to early January:
   - It is dark by 16:00–16:30, so weekday test rolls are impossible and every test rides on weekends.
   - Outdoor flea markets shrink, and fotoburza events are sparse.
   - Christmas eats week 11–12 time.
   - The one upside: first listings hit Advent gift demand.
2. **The untested backlog.** The real failure mode for flippers is buying faster than testing (buying is the fun part). Weeks 4 and 10 buy 20–30 units at a processing capacity of about 4–5 units/week, which builds a 4–6-week backlog of cash sitting in untested boxes.

**Verdict on the plan:** executable over about **16–20 weeks**, not 12. Re-baseline the gates to "days since first listing" and add a backlog cap.

## 7. Sourcing fragility, skill ramp and time to first sale

**Sourcing** (the binding constraint)
- The dossier's own §4 shows **negative or thin spreads on everything Czech sellers can already price**: mju I, tested Praktica sets, mju II. The CZ media habit of "your old X is worth a fortune" articles (06-verification, on glass) applies to cameras too.
- The ≤30%-of-exit rule is sound, but it will reject most listings. The hours go into screening alerts and haggling that don't close. The dossier budgets 30 min of sourcing per camera; for an employee I estimate **45–75 min per sellable unit**, including lost deals [estimate].
- **Daytime disadvantage.** Good Bazoš/Vinted/Aukro deals go within hours. A founder in a 9–5 job reacts in the evening.
- **Japanese SLRs are scarce in CZ** (the dossier's own point), and the best SKUs are marked "thin supply" or "rare".
- **Implication:** the realistic sustained yield is **10–14 sellable units/month**, not 16–24 [estimate]. It is lens-heavy early, because lenses are what can legally be sold abroad in stage 1.

**Skill ramp** (the dossier's §8a is realistic; three additions)
1. **Bulk-loaded reusable cassettes leak light and scratch film.** That contaminates the very test that decides "light-leak-free". Auto-rewinding compacts (mju, Big Mini, Espio) also swallow the leader, so each compact test consumes a factory roll. Budget **+€4–6 per compact**.
2. **"Sample scans" with a NEX-6 need a macro setup:** an adapted enlarger or macro lens, tubes and a film holder. That is €50–100 not in the stage-1 budget.
3. **Fungus hygiene.** Store incoming lenses separately in a dry box (€30–60) so one fungal lot doesn't infect the stock.
- **Time to competence:** diagnosis and seals in 20–40 h (agreed). CLA is 50–100 h more, and stage 1 should sell "tested and cleaned" only (agreed).

**Time to first sale**
- The IČO takes about 5 working days, then the eBay.de business setup and identified-person registration.
- The first lens listings go live in weeks 5–6, and the first sale comes around **week 6–7**.
- First cash (after eBay's new-seller holds) arrives around **week 8–10**.
- The first CZ camera sale (Bazoš/Aukro) can come in week 5.
- The first DE camera sale comes around **month 4–5**, after WEEE registration.

## 9. Fatal flaws

None is legally fatal. Two are fatal **for the brief**, which asks for an asymmetric, supply-constrained edge that actually pays:
1. **No asymmetry.**
   - The "edge" is testing labour. The CZ access edge is negative on priced items (the dossier's own §4, scorer B, 02-evidence).
   - The margin is a wage for tinkering, at €7–13/h depending on whose numbers you use.
   - The ceiling is hours: at 15 h/week and about 3 h per camera, roughly 20 units a month is the physical maximum.
2. **The best edge (EU-shipped, VAT-free) is legally available to only one line in stage 1, and to DE only thereafter.**
   - Three of the four product lines are EEE, and every extra EU country needs its own WEEE registration.
   - Above €10k of EU sales the edge survives only through the SME scheme.
   - So the positioning in §5 ("EU-wide") is mostly unavailable for cameras.

## 11. Corrected month-12 estimate (month 12 is late 2027; pre-tax; assumes the founder is still running it)

**Realistic: ≈ €340/month**
- **Units:** 12 a month: 7 cameras (compacts in CZ + DE; SLR kits to DE after WEEE) and 5 lens kits. This sits between the dossier's pessimistic 8 and realistic 16, given §7.
- **Net per unit ≈ €38**, starting from the dossier's blended ≈ €41:
  - +€3 for moving WEEE out of the per-unit cost and into fixed costs
  - −€1 for adapters at €15–20
  - −€1 for factory-roll compact tests
  - −€3 for a 8–12% return/INAD reserve on 24-month DE exposure
  - −€1 for FX and fee uncertainty
- **Fixed costs ≈ €115/month:** WEEE €70–90, legal texts €12, LUCID and dual system €5, advisor and SME filings €15, tool write-offs €10.
- **Profit:** 12 × €38 − €115 = **≈ €340/month**.
- **Hours ≈ 44/month (≈ 10 h/week):**
  - 7 cameras × 3.3 h = 23 h
  - 5 lens kits × 1.8 h = 9 h
  - admin 5 h
  - unattributed sourcing (alerts, lost deals, one flea market) 7 h
- That gives **≈ €8/h pre-tax**.
- About €4.1k/yr ≈ 100k CZK stays **under the 117,521 CZK social cliff**, so levies are about 21.75%. That is **≈ €265/month after tax, ≈ €6/h**.

| **Realistic** | 12 × €38 − €115 | **≈ €340** | ≈ 44 | **≈ €8** |
| **Optimistic** (a clearance supplier works; premium finds) | 20 × €45 − €130 | **≈ €770** | ≈ 65 (at the 15 h/week cap) | ≈ €12 |
| *Dossier* | *16 × €41 − €60* | *€600* | *≈ 41–47* | *claims €15–20; its own numbers give ≈ €13* |

The optimistic case crosses the social cliff: 37.8% levies leave about €480 net. Doubling beyond it is impossible within 15 h/week without cutting the per-camera time.

---

## 12. Would you tell a friend to do this?

**Only if** they already shoot film, enjoy fixing cameras, and would spend the evenings on it anyway. In that case, run this version:
1. Lens-first, on eBay.de.
2. Cameras CZ-only until 10 lens sales prove the channel.
3. AML/tax purchase slips from day one.
4. The €990 test with its gates counted from the first listing, and a backlog cap.
5. Expect about €5–10/h after tax.

**For a founder who has been burned by ideas that "sounded good" and wants money rather than a hobby: no.** It will make some money. It won't make asymmetric money. The ceiling is the founder's hours, and the legal overhead grows with every EU country added.
