## 1. Verdict

**VIABLE (marginal): score 5/10.** Worth doing only as a capped test running to the end of January.

- **Execution is the strongest part of this idea.** It has the lightest legal load of any physical candidate: no hallmarking, no WEEE, no CE. The €1,000 test caps the downside, and unsold stock resells in CZ.
- **The plan, as written, will not run inside 10–15 h/week.** Weeks 3–5 need about 20–28 h/week at beginner speeds (section 6). The dossier's time model also undercounts sourcing hours by a factor of 2–3 (section 7).
- **The dossier misses two compliance items:**
  1. **AML identification.** A used-goods trader must identify the seller on *every* purchase of used goods, whatever the value. This collides with anonymous flea-market and Bazoš buying.
  2. **German Abmahnung exposure on eBay.de.** German competitors and law firms can send costly cease-and-desist letters over faulty legal texts. Neither item is fatal, and both are cheap to fix.
- **My corrected realistic month-12 result is about €330/month before tax, at about €7/hour.** The dossier says €575/month at €15–20/h. Being able to execute the plan does not make the money worth the hours.

## 5. The EU-first strategy against the €10k OSS ceiling (a structural cap)

**Where the threshold sits** [computed]:
- €10,000/yr ÷ €64 average order (item plus shipping) ≈ **13 EU B2C orders a month**.

**Where the realistic case lands:**
- At my realistic 20 sales/month with 55–65% EU buyers, that is 11–13 EU orders/month, or **€8.4–10k/yr**, just under the line.

**What crossing it costs:**
- Every EU order above the line loses about €10–11 of destination VAT. That is about **35% of unit net**.
- The dossier's "route volume to UK/US/CZ" lever doesn't exist on marketplaces. UK volume is small, and US volume is impaired and demoted in search.

**Consequences:**
- The optimistic case (45 sales/month) and any year-2 growth hit an automatic margin cut.
- The only escape is voluntary VAT registration with the § 90 margin scheme. That brings full VAT-payer bookkeeping and control statements (kontrolní hlášení), which is not worth it below about 30 EU orders/month (the dossier agrees).
- **This business has a soft ceiling of roughly €600–800/month profit** for a non-VAT-payer side business. That is a legal-structure ceiling, not a demand ceiling.

## 7. Sourcing fragility and the skill ramp

**Sourcing:**
- Sourcing is the *least* fragile element for common stock and the *most* fragile for the top tier (lines B and E, 1–3 finds a month per the dossier), which carries the upside.
- Specific weaknesses:
  - Kolbenova and other flea-market dealers know Sklo Union prices after the 2026 media hype, so real bargains need a 6–7 am arrival.
  - Bazoš lots are contested by other pickers with instant alerts.
  - Sellers of cheap glass often refuse to ship fragile items, which means in-person pickups at 1–2 h each.
  - Vyklízení firms already have dealer relationships, so a newcomer gets the leftovers for the first months.
  - Outdoor flea markets thin out in Jan–Mar.
- The new AML identification duty makes anonymous purchases from private sellers non-compliant.
- None of this kills supply. It raises the average buy to about €10–14 and the sourcing minutes per sold unit to 35–60.

**Skill ramp** (all learnable, but it takes months, not weeks):
1. **Identification:** factory, designer and catalogue number. Expect 10–20% uncertain or misattributed pieces in the first 3 months [estimate]. Use "attributed to" wording and collector Facebook groups.
2. **Condition grading:** fleabites, "sick" glass, polished rims, UV checks. Missed damage is the main source of "not as described" refunds.
3. **Dating:** vintage versus reissue, for Etsy's 20-year rule.
4. **Pricing from sold data, not asks.** Use eBay Terapeak, which is free in Seller Hub [knowledge]. Without sold data the "≥ 4× buy" rule is guesswork. Budget WorthPoint only if it pays back.
5. **Glass photography:** backlit white acrylic, plus about €50 more than the dossier's light tent.
6. **Double-box packing:** about 1 week to learn.

**Storage (not mentioned in the dossier):**
- 350–400 active listings of vases and bowls need roughly 20–25 m of 40 cm-deep shelving, plus 1–2 m³ of boxes and bubble wrap [estimate].
- In a typical Czech flat this is a real constraint, and a household-friction risk.
- Cap active stock at what fits; about 250 is probably the practical limit.

## 8. What kills this in the first 6 months (ranked)

The probabilities below are my estimates, and they overlap.

1. **Sell-through below the gates (≈ 40–50%).**
   - EU-only demand is unmeasured, new shops are invisible on Etsy, and €12–13 shipping from CZ competes against German private sellers of the same glass.
   - The dossier's day-90 rule ("December < 12 sales → kill") will probably trigger.
   - This is a *correct* kill, and the loss is €200–400 plus about 130 h.
2. **Time overrun and fatigue in Oct–Dec (≈ 25%).** Launch overlaps the Q4 peak and the holidays, and weeks 3–5 need 20–28 h.
3. **An account event during the single Q4 window (≈ 15%).** Etsy KYC, suspension or reserves, or eBay new-seller limits, delay a channel by 2–4 weeks and Q4 is gone.
4. **Breakage and "not as described" clusters from beginner packing and grading (≈ 10–15%, rarely fatal alone).** Refunds, bad reviews, and account-health penalties that cut visibility.
5. **Admin and legal shocks (≈ 5%, rarely fatal).** A missed identified-person VAT return, a German Abmahnung, or an AML complaint.

## 9. Fatal flaws

**No fatal execution, legal or platform flaw.** Compliance is light and manageable, and the capital at risk is small and recoverable.

**Two near-fatal issues:**
1. **The economics of the founder's hour** (≈ €7/h realistic, section 11), combined with a ~50% chance of failing the gates. This is not a "demand clearly bigger than supply" business. It is a picker's grind with a low ceiling.
2. **The launch plan's workload is incompatible with the brief's 10–15 h/week** in weeks 3–5. Unless it is re-scheduled, the test measures the founder's stamina, not the market.

## 12. Would you tell a friend to do this?

**Only if** all of the following hold:
- They already enjoy flea markets and Czech mid-century design, so a good part of the sourcing hours is leisure.
- They have storage space.
- They accept that the realistic outcome is about €300/month at about €7/h.
- They run it strictly as a **≤ €1,000 test to the end of January**, with the re-scheduled plan (F1), purchase slips from day 1 (F2), a German legal-text service before the first eBay.de listing (F3), and no add-on lines before the gate.

If they want a business where demand clearly outruns supply and pays well per hour, **no**. Execution and legal risk are low, which is why this ranked first. But low risk is not the same thing as a good return.
