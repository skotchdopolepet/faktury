# Review 01, ECONOMICS lens: vintage Czechoslovak glass picker (C02)

Reviewer: unit-economics and time skeptic. Date: 2026-09-29. Dossier reviewed: `dossiers/01-vintage-czech-glass.md`.
Inputs read: `00-brief.md`, the dossier, `02-main-loop-evidence.md`, `06-verification.md`, `cz-legal-tax-quickstart.md` (§§ 0–3, 5) and the C02 rows in `scores/scorer-A/B/C.md`.
Tags: **[verified: URL]** is a search-result snippet; **[knowledge]** is background knowledge not re-checked this session; **[estimate]** is my arithmetic, with the reasoning shown. Rate: 24.5 CZK/EUR.

## 1. Verdict

**WEAK: 4/10.**

- The idea does make money, and the downside is small, because unsold stock can be resold in CZ. But the dossier's economics do not hold up once its own numbers are applied consistently.
  - The dossier's time model charges only about 20 min of sourcing and no processing time for listed items that never sell.
  - Its unit table prices a Rosice vase at the midpoint of *asking* prices and leaves out onsite Etsy Ads.
- Corrected, a typical sale nets about **€19–21** and really costs about **2–2.5 hours**.
- The realistic month-12 profit is about **€275/month before levies (≈ €215 after)**, at roughly **€6/h**. That is close to the Czech minimum wage and a third of the dossier's €15–20/h.
- The ceiling is about **€500–700/month** before levies. Three limits bind together at about 25–30 sales a month:
  - the 15 h/week time cap;
  - the €10k EU distance-sales VAT threshold;
  - the social-insurance cliff.

## 6. Sell-through, cash tied up in inventory, year-1 cash

- **Sell-through:** 18 sales from ~300 active listings is **≈ 6%/month**, below the dossier's own day-90 gate of 8%. Inventory turns about 0.7×/yr, which means **≈ 16–17 months of stock cover**.
- **Stock at cost at month 12:** 300 pieces × ≈ €8.5, plus 5–10 E-grade pieces × €40, comes to **≈ €2,800–3,000** [estimate]. The dossier's stage plan implies a similar figure.
- **Year-1 cash:**
  - Summed monthly profit, month 1 to month 12, is ≈ **€1,650 before levies**, ramping from about −€150 in October to ≈ €275 in September 2027 [estimate].
  - Subtract one-off setup of ≈ €450 and €2,900 of stock on hand.
  - The founder is **≈ €1,700 out of pocket at month 12**, with that money sitting in slow stock.
- **Year-1 hours:** ≈ 450–550, giving an effective **≈ €3–4/h in year 1**.
- **Return on capital is fine.** ≈ €3.3k/yr on ≈ €3.5k deployed. **Capital is not the problem; time is.**

## 7. Fatal flaws

For the brief's purpose ("it must actually make money" at 10–15 h/week), there is one fatal flaw and one ceiling. Neither is fatal in the sense of losing money.

1. **The €/hour is structurally too low.**
   - Each typical sale nets ≈ €19–21 but costs ≈ 2–2.5 h of real time once sourcing and unsold listings are counted.
   - The only lever that fixes this is a bigger share of lines B and E (≥ €80 items). That line is supply-limited to about 1–3 finds a month, and informed CZ collectors compete for it: 06 shows attributed pieces at ~4,000 CZK on Aukro, and media hype inflates what private sellers ask.
   - Low-value volume cannot fix the problem, because packing, postage and handling are fixed per parcel.
2. **The ceiling is low and three limits converge on it.** Together they cap the business at **≈ €500–700/month before levies (≈ €400 after)**, whatever the execution:
   - the time cap at ~30–35 sales a month;
   - the €10k EU distance-sales threshold at ~26 sales a month (55% EU share);
   - the social-insurance dead zone at €400–500/month profit.

## 8. Fixable issues and fixes

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Price assumptions are built on asking prices | Price and buy from **sold** data: eBay sold/completed listings, Catawiki's past results and Etsy sold items. Log the realized price for every sale and re-set the buy rule monthly |
| Cheap lines dilute €/h (line C at ≈ €12, ornaments at ≈ €10–17) | Drop line C. Raise the buy floor to **expected sale ≥ €50 and ≥ 5× cost**. Sell ornaments only as Q4 bundles bought at ≤ €1/pc. Send sub-€35 finds straight to a monthly CZ lot sale (Aukro/Bazoš) rather than listing them |
| The time model counts only sold units | Log **all** hours from day 1: sourcing, unlisted rejects and unsold listings. Put a **€/h gate** in the kill criteria: **net profit ÷ total hours logged ≥ €10/h by day 90, else stop**. The day-60 "≤ 100 min per sold unit" test must use total hours |
| Onsite ads and renewals are missing from the unit table | Budget €1–1.5/day in Etsy Ads from week 3 and measure ROAS. Relist or retire any piece unsold after 6 months instead of renewing it indefinitely |
| Stock builds faster than it sells (≈ 16 months of cover) | Cap active listings at **~150–200** until measured sell-through is ≥ 8%/month. That frees ≈ €1,000–1,500 of cash |
| Postage and packaging are each ≈ 20% of the order | Offer Packeta **pickup-point** delivery in DE/AT where it is available (cheaper than home delivery [knowledge]). Reuse clean second-hand boxes and buy fill in bulk, aiming for **€1.5–2 of packaging per parcel**. Get a written Packeta answer on glass compensation before scaling |
| The EUR payout assumption | Open a EUR account (Fio, Revolut Business or Wise) as Etsy's payout account to avoid the 2.5% conversion fee |
| VAT and social cliffs | Track EU B2C sales monthly against €10k. Keep annual profit **either ≤ €4.8k or clearly ≥ €6k**, and use year-end inventory purchases (deductible when paid under actual expenses) to stay below the cliff |
| Double selling across Etsy and eBay | Either use a sync tool (≈ €20–30/month, which is only worth it above ~25 sales/month) or list each piece on **one** channel only |

## 10. Would you tell a friend to do this?

**Only if** they already enjoy flea markets and Czech glass and would hunt anyway, and they accept roughly €5–10/h as a paid hobby. In that case:
- run it as a ≤ €1,000, high-floor shop (expected sale ≥ €50);
- keep it to one Saturday a week;
- apply a hard €10/h gate at day 90.

**As the brief's "asymmetric, actually makes money" side business: no.** The origin asymmetry is real. But after fees, a 2–3 kg double-boxed parcel, EU-only demand and 2+ hours per sale, most of the spread goes to postage, platform fees and the founder's evenings.
