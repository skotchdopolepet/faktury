# Scorer C: Czech side-e-shop operator lens

Date: 2026-09-29. Inputs: `00-brief.md`, `01-longlist.md`, `02-main-loop-evidence.md` (overrides hunter claims where they conflict) and `cz-legal-tax-quickstart.md`. I had no web access. Tags: **[K]** = my own operator knowledge, not verified this session; **[E]** = estimate with the reasoning inline; anything else comes from the files above.

Scale is 1–10. budget_risk: 10 = cheap to test, low downside. Reliability means "a competent beginner reaches €500+/month profit within 12 months". I cap that at 6, because the Etsy base rate is harsh: about 14% of shops clear $1,000/month profit (02-evidence). Nothing on this list has evidence strong enough to justify 7+.

## Operator notes that move many scores

- **The identified-person trap hits every Etsy, eBay, Vinted, Allegro, Kaufland, Meta or Google line.** In practice that means a VAT return and 21% on the fees almost every month, about 20–30 min/month of admin, plus a penalty risk if you forget [K + quickstart §3.3]. Lines that sell through Czech platforms avoid it: Bazoš, Aukro, the Shoptet add-on store, Heureka, and an own shop on a Czech payment gateway. This is a small but real cz_fit advantage for C05, C13, C12 (Bazoš), C22 and C08.
- **Refurbished electronics into DE is not "just list on eBay.de".** Since ElektroG3 (2023), German marketplaces must check the WEEE registration of EEE sellers, and a CZ seller needs a stiftung ear authorised representative. Fitting a new Li-ion cell adds battery EPR [K]. That drags C01, C29 and the whole-device part of C12 down unless they start CZ/SK-only.
- **Glass and framed goods by Packeta:** you need to double-box, and compensation for breakage is weak unless the packing is exemplary [K]. Budget 1–3% breakage plus 5–10 min of packing per item.
- **Bazoš realities:** haggling ("dám 500"), no-shows and personal pickups eat evenings. Every resale line needs 2–4 h/week of pure sourcing logistics on top of the hunter "per unit" times [K].
- **Precious metal:** anyone trading precious-metal articles must notify the Puncovní úřad before starting and deal only in hallmarked pieces. Buying gold or silver from private individuals brings record-keeping and AML-style identification duties [K; quickstart §4.8]. That affects C06 and C24, and C03/C04 once they involve silver.
- **Evenings reality:** B2B software support (C05, C13) gets its questions at 10:00 on a Tuesday. Resale and made-to-stock goods can be answered at 21:00. Merchants tolerate a 24 h email SLA, but an accounting sync that breaks on the 24th (kontrolní hlášení week) will not wait [K].

## My top 5, in order

1. **C02: Vintage Czechoslovak glass and ceramics picker.** Two CZ-based Etsy shops with 1,000+ reviews prove the exact model from here, and the raw supply (flea markets, house clearances, "po babičce" lots) exists in real volume, not just in theory. It has the lightest regulatory load of any physical idea and inventory that resells domestically if it fails. Price for EU buyers first (DE, NL, UK via Etsy), add eBay.de for liquidity, and be ruthless about double-boxing and per-item time.

2. **C07: Tested film cameras and lens kits.** It is a liquid, rising market (02 evidence), and "serviced/tested" carries a measurable ~2× premium over as-is. Sticking to mechanical bodies keeps it out of WEEE. Buy only untested lots and estate boxes, never hyped singles at CZ asks, and run it off the same sourcing trips as C02 so one Saturday feeds both lines.

3. **C01: Flash-modded iPods.** Demand and backlog evidence is the strongest on the list, and a broken-donor-to-finished spread exists. But plan on €150–200 realised prices, not €230, and expect dud donors. Start CZ/SK (Bazoš, Aukro, an own shop) plus Etsy EU. Don't touch eBay.de until you have priced the stiftung ear and battery authorised-representative costs, which could eat a thin month's profit.

4. **C05: Shoptet promo-mechanics add-on.** This only applies if the founder can code (or ship with AI help) and a two-evening API spike proves that gift tiers and X+Y can actually modify the cart. If so, it is the best risk profile here: almost no cash at risk, a built-in storefront full of merchants already paying 100–300 CZK/month for badly rated native tools, and a purely Czech legal footprint with no identified-person trigger. It is a slow compounder rather than a quick €500.

5. **C12: DDR4/SSD part-out.** This is a time-boxed window play, not a business to keep. Demand is certain, and selling components locally on Bazoš avoids WEEE, Etsy fees and VAT admin. Run the 40-listing buy-rule test first. If fewer than a quarter of whole-PC listings clear it, walk away, because the lag has closed.

Near misses: **C04** has the best hourly money and a real queue, but it is custom work on irreplaceable client material, which is at odds with the no-service rule, and one ruined sample will spread through CZ mum groups. **C03** fits CZ perfectly, but its upside is capped (the best CZ Etsy shop has 533 lifetime sales). It works best as an add-on line to C02 on the same Etsy shop, not as the main business.

## Ideas I would veto and why

- **C09 Kydex holsters.** The claimed gap is false: incumbents ship in about a week (02 evidence), the licence-holder count is falling, ads are restricted, and there is retention liability. You would be buying €1,500–3,500 of tooling to enter a saturated market.
- **C16 Jawa/ČZ parts on eBay.** DE/NL/CZ exporters with 9,000–14,000 SKUs already serve it. A €10 net per order with slow stock never reaches €500/month in evenings.
- **C30 Lead-free sinkers.** The forced demand lands mostly after the 12-month window, and the category will be flooded by CZ carp brands and Chinese tungsten at €1–3 net per pack. Home metal casting on top.
- **C10 HAN reader, in its current form.** It makes the founder a radio-equipment manufacturer (RED plus the EN 18031 cyber requirement, EMC, WEEE) for a niche with zero paying-demand evidence. Pillar-box meters and inverter meters shrink the market further. Revisit only if a CZ seller's sales numbers appear.
- **C23 CLP label packs.** It is a compliance service in disguise, with liability for wrong labels, suppliers give the data away, and there is no paid demand.
- **C33 Kniha jízd spreadsheet.** The target users mostly don't need a logbook (paušál or the flat car deduction), and free templates are everywhere.
- **C31 Merchant-licence club and C35 TRX-4M parts.** They sell to people who print their own, into categories already saturated, with no CZ edge. The Etsy own-design rule makes C31 worse.
- **C34 Aquarium botanicals.** A commodity with a €3–5 net per pack. You would need 100+ packs a month plus seasonal foraging to reach the target.
- **C29 Modded Game Boy.** It has the WEEE and battery burden of C01 but none of its sourcing edge or supply gap.
