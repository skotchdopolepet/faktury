# Scorer A: pragmatic e-commerce operator

**Persona.** 8 years running profitable Etsy, eBay and Vinted shops out of Central Europe. I have watched hundreds of side hustles die. These are the patterns I have seen fail:
- made-to-order craft that eats every evening;
- new "brands" on Etsy with no traffic;
- digital products with no distribution;
- 3D-print niches the buyers can print themselves.

The pattern I have seen work is **buying under-priced stock that has visible foreign sold comps, adding a real value step (test, clean, fix, document) and selling it cross-border on a liquid marketplace.**

**Inputs:**
- `00-brief.md`;
- `01-longlist.md`;
- `02-main-loop-evidence.md`, which overrides the hunters where they conflict. Its overrides cover Kydex lead times, Jawa supply, TM7 saturation, the Shoptet promo add-ons already existing, the shorter breast-milk turnaround in DE, CZ moldavite dealers already exporting, the CZ film-camera asks, the German iPod modders and the $3.75/pc ornament lots.

I had no web access. Tags: **[knowledge]** is my operator experience, not checked this session; **[estimate]** is my own arithmetic.

**Scale:** 1 = terrible, 10 = excellent.
- **demand:** proof people are paying now.
- **asym:** demand > supply, or a structural gap.
- **econ:** net per unit and per hour after fees and shipping.
- **solo:** fits 10–15 h/week of light work, and is not a service.
- **rel:** chance that a competent beginner reaches €500+/month profit within 12 months. As a rough guide, 6 ≈ 40–50%, 4 ≈ 25%, 2 ≈ <10%.
- **budget:** 10 = cheap test, low downside.
- **cz:** a real CZ edge plus a light regulatory load.

The total is an unweighted sum out of 70, for orientation only.

## My top 5, in order

1. **C02: Vintage Czechoslovak glass and ceramics picker (Σ 52).** It is the only candidate where the *exact* model is already proven by CZ-based shops with more than 1,000 reviews each, and the downside is almost nil because unsold stock resells locally at cost. Start with EU buyers on eBay.de and Etsy, plus Pamono for attributed pieces. Price ornaments as bundles only, and treat breakage-proof packing as a core skill, not an afterthought.
2. **C01: Flash-modded iPod Classic (Σ 44).** The value step is transformational: a €10–25 dead donor becomes a €160–200 product, and demand is rising and press-verified. Expect German competition and prices nearer $120–160 than the hunter's €230. Before selling into DE, budget roughly €300–600 a year for an authorised representative to handle WEEE and battery registration [knowledge, estimate]; that cost is small next to the margin.
3. **C07: Tested film cameras + adapted lens kits (Σ 45).** This is the deepest and most liquid used-goods market on the list, and it runs on the same Bazoš, estate-lot and flea-market trips as C02. Buy only untested and estate lots, never tested sets that already ask ≈ €125. Test every body with film before listing, because the ~2× premium for "serviced" is the whole margin. I rank it below C01 only because CZ asks already sit close to exit prices.
4. **C12: DDR4/SSD part-out (Σ 45).** Scarcity and paying demand are better documented here than anywhere else, the work is the lightest on the list, and RAM sells within days, so inventory risk is controllable. Everything hinges on whole-device sellers lagging the RAM spike, so run the €0 test (log 40 listings against DE sold prices) before buying anything. Treat it as a 12–18-month cash window that funds the lasting business, not as the business itself.
5. **C06: Vintage Bohemian garnet jewelry (Σ 43).** Its main merit is running on the same sourcing loop as C02 and C07: the same bazaars, pawnshops and estate lots, one more high-ticket category. On its own it is weaker: slow sell-through, fakes, a US-heavy buyer base and Assay Office duties. Buy only pieces you can hallmark-verify, and cap stock at €1,000 until 10 pieces have sold.

**Operator's note on the top 5.** Items 1, 3 and 5, and the donor side of 2, are **one business**: a "Czech estate picker" with several exit categories. Run it as one shop per channel, with one sourcing calendar and one packing station. Diversifying across categories is how pickers survive the months when one category stalls [knowledge].

**Honourable mention, conditional:** C05 and C13 (Shoptet add-ons) are the only digital ideas with a real distribution channel, a marketplace where merchants search and pay. They would enter my top 5 only if the founder is an experienced PHP/JS developer. Of the two, C13 now looks better than C05, because the evidence file shows the promo space is already crowded.

## Ideas I would veto and why

- **C04, breast-milk jewelry: veto.** It is a service in disguise: custom work on irreplaceable client material, with deadlines and heavy messaging that don't fit a day job. The EU turnaround gap has closed (DE makers quote 2–6 weeks, and DIY kits already exist). A new shop with no reviews won't be trusted with breast milk, and silver needs a craft licence. One ruined piece means a 1-star review in a word-of-mouth niche.
- **C09, Kydex holsters: veto.** The claimed supply gap does not exist: incumbents ship in about a week, and the manufacturer sells its own. The licence-holder base is shrinking. The workshop brings dust, noise and liability, and ad platforms block the category.
- **C15, yarn advent calendar: veto for this 12-month window.** The 2026 pre-order season is already over, so the first real revenue would land in Nov 2027. The proof comes from dyers who spent years building an audience, and a new dyer has none.
- **C16, Jawa/ČZ parts: veto.** Specialists with 9,000–14,000 SKUs, their own reproductions and CNC shops already serve the market. A reseller would earn about €10 an order on slow stock.
- **C23, CLP label packs: veto.** Nobody is shown paying. The founder would take legal liability for other people's chemical classification, which is exactly the "regulation creates a service, not a product" trap.
- **C30, lead-free sinkers: veto.** The forced demand arrives around 2029, earnings are €1–3 a pack, and large brands will own the category.
- **C31, merchant-licence design club: veto.** Etsy's June 2025 own-design rule removes the core buyers, and piracy kills licence enforcement.
- **C33, kniha jízd spreadsheet: veto.** No evidence anyone pays, and it is beaten by the flat deduction, free templates and accounting modules.
- **C24, minimalist garnet brand: soft veto.** The brief uses it as the model of a good offer, but there is no evidence of an origin premium. It is a new brand in Etsy's most saturated category with €1.5–3.5k of stock designs. Reconsider only after C06 proves that buyers pay extra for "genuine Bohemian".
- **C21, C34 and C35: veto as primary businesses.** Each is hobby money at best: pennies per download, €3–5 per pack, or buyers who print their own parts. None can reach €500/month in 12 months at 10–15 h/week.
