# Scorer B: skeptical investor (base rates, downside protection, asymmetric bets)

Date: 2026-09-29. Inputs: `00-brief.md`, `01-longlist.md` and `02-main-loop-evidence.md`. Where the orchestrator's evidence conflicts with a hunter's claim, the orchestrator wins. I did no web research; judgements from my own background are tagged **[knowledge]**.

## How I scored
- **Base rate.** The median Etsy shop grosses about $574/month, and only about 14% clear $1,000/month profit (02-evidence). A part-timer netting €500+/month within 12 months is therefore the exception, not the default. On **reliability**, 6 means a roughly 35–40% chance for a competent beginner, 4 roughly 20%, and 2 or less means under 10%.
- **What earns points.** Completed transactions: reviews, sold counts, ratings on paid products, and prices that have visibly moved. Favourites, market pages, "listings exist" and seller marketing count for little.
- **Asymmetry means durability.** A gap that any CAD user, flipper or the incumbent can close within 6–12 months scores 1–3. It scores high only when supply is structurally stuck: no new production, geology, origin, or a small-language moat.
- **Evidence caveat.** Nothing in this swarm was read first-hand; everything is a search snippet. I discount single-source and seller-published numbers.

## My top 5, in order

1. **C02: Vintage Czechoslovak glass picker.** This is the only idea where a CZ-shipping shop has thousands of completed sales doing exactly this. The asymmetry is structural: the goods were made here, domestic demand is lower, and estates keep renewing supply. That has held for more than 10 years and won't close in 6–12 months. Stock resells locally at close to cost, so the downside is capped at a few hundred euros of time. Cap buy-in per piece and track sell-through by category from month 1.
2. **C07: Tested film cameras + lens kits.** It has the deepest and most liquid used market of any candidate. 02-evidence confirms prices rose through 2025, and no new bodies are made, so supply can't catch up. The margin is locked in at purchase against global comps, and inventory can be liquidated on eBay within weeks. Buy only untested lots and non-hyped bodies, and accept that the CZ edge is sourcing effort, not a moat.
3. **C03: Moldavite with provenance.** Geology is the most durable supply constraint in the list. The product is tiny, light, needs no labour and resells at close to cost. Two caveats: the realistic upside is capped (the top CZ Etsy seller has 533 lifetime sales), and fad decay is the main risk. Test with €500 of invoiced stones before scaling.
4. **C05: Shoptet promo add-on.** Cash at risk is near zero, revenue is recurring and sticky, and the demand evidence is the best-quality dataset in the swarm: paid native add-ons with poor ratings and no third-party rival. Before writing a line of code, read the partner documentation to confirm an add-on can change cart prices. If it can't, the idea is dead, and that check costs an evening.
5. **C06: Vintage Bohemian garnet jewelry.** It uses the same sourcing loop as C02, and gold and silver pieces carry a metal-value floor, which is real downside protection. The spread is unproven (asking prices on both sides), and fakes punish beginners. Run it only as a side line of C02, starting with hallmarked silver and gold, never unmarked tombak.

Near misses: C01 is the best-evidenced *fad*. Trade it opportunistically, but don't build on it, because the price anchor is already lower than the hunter assumed and German modders are active. C12 is a real but explicitly temporary window. C04 has strong unit economics but sits on the service line.

## Ideas I would veto and why

- **C09 Kydex holsters.** Fresh evidence falsifies the premise: incumbents ship in about a week, the factory's own shop sells holsters, and licence holders are declining.
- **C16 Jawa/ČZ parts.** Well served by exporters with 9,000–14,000 SKUs. Margins are thin and the owner base is ageing.
- **C14 TM7 organisers.** Supply is already responding, the only barrier is CAD, and there is no CZ edge.
- **C30 Lead-free sinkers.** The forced demand arrives after the 12-month window, units are €1–3, and big brands will take it.
- **C12 as a business** (acceptable only as a hard-capped trade). The RAM spike is a time-boxed window, the buy-side lag is unverified, and inventory is exposed to a price reversal.
- **C08 A2 exam prep.** Official new-format model tests and free AI-graded practice already exist, and willingness to pay for a PDF pack is unproven.
- **C13 accounting connector.** The gap is probably illusory because off-marketplace integrators exist [knowledge], and urgent accounting support clashes with a day job.
- **C21, C22, C23, C31 and C33 (zero-marginal-cost digital).** Free substitutes, power-law payouts and no sales evidence. This is the founder's own documented failure mode.
- **C28, C29, C35 and C24.** No paid demand for this specific offer (C28, C24), or a crowded field with no edge (C29, C35).
- **C15, C19 and C20 as primary bets.** Q4 seasonality breaks the 15 h/week cap, and none shows a measured gap.
