## 1. Why the swarm came back with modest ideas

1. **The swarm searched where evidence was easy to find, and easy-to-find markets are crowded.** The hunters searched English marketplaces (Etsy, eBay, Pamono, Kleinanzeigen), where a market shows up as many listings. Many listings means a lot of supply, so the method selected contested markets. Markets where demand really exceeds supply leave few traces on those sites. They show up instead in:
   - Czech Facebook groups;
   - the "Koupím" (wanted) sections of Bazoš and Aukro;
   - municipal purchase orders;
   - deposit books and pre-order lists;
   - Discogs and BrickLink want lists.

   None of these were searched.
2. **The swarm measured supply and called it demand.** "Demand > supply" was tested with asking prices and listing counts. Both describe supply. Direct measures of demand exist but went unused:
   - deposits taken before the product exists;
   - Discogs *Want* against *Have* counts;
   - "Koupím" ads compared with offers;
   - waitlists that have a length.
3. **Every lens was a product type or a channel.** The 11 lenses covered refurb, handmade, digital, parts, consumables and so on. None was a *buyer* lens (municipalities, volunteer fire brigades, folk ensembles, hunting associations, schools). None was a *mechanism* lens: exclusive rights, pre-sale, biological lead time, want-list marketplaces.
4. **Skill was scored as a risk, but skill is the only thing that keeps supply short.** "Solo feasibility" pulled down every idea that needed a craft or a skill. Ideas that need no skill (picking, part-outs, printables) are crowded almost by definition: anyone can enter, so everyone has. The swarm also never asked what the founder can already do. C05 was killed partly because "coding ability is unknown". A 10-question skills check would re-rank the whole list.
5. **A labour-bound model has a hard income ceiling.** 12 h/week is about 52 h/month. At the €10–20/h the reviewers found, that caps profit at roughly **€500–1,000/month whatever the idea**. That is why every survivor sits at €100–700. To break the ceiling, supply has to grow without the founder's hours, through one of four routes:
   - other people's labour (partner-made goods, licences);
   - biology (colonies and trees multiply);
   - repeat B2B orders;
   - software.
6. **Only a supply that can't react within a season is structurally short.** In the 35 candidates, only C04 (craft capacity) and C15 (a capped pre-order) had any shortage built into their structure. The best structural sources are:
   - **biological lead time**: bees and grafted trees can't be made faster than one season;
   - **exclusive rights**: a licence or distribution contract;
   - **finite stock sold into a live want list**;
   - **craft bottlenecks the founder can route around** by partnering instead of doing the craft.
7. **The export frame broke, and domestic demand was never looked at.** The end of US de minimis hurt the export-picker model (06-verification). Czech domestic buyers with Czech-only needs were barely examined, apart from C08, C10, C22 and C33.

## 2. New candidates (none is in the longlist)

Asymmetry type key: **S** = structural shortage (supply can't respond), **E** = exclusivity, **D** = distribution gap (the buyers exist but sellers don't show up; this is not scarcity).

### N1: "Vítání občánků" (baby-welcome ceremony) and anniversary kits personalised with the municipality's registered coat of arms, sold B2B to Czech municipalities (type D, recurring)
- **Offer.** A standard kit with only the crest and name changed, so it stays a product and not a design service:
  - a laser-engraved beech keepsake box;
  - an archival-paper *pamětní list* (commemorative certificate) printed with the crest;
  - an engraved stainless or silver-plated spoon. Plated silver avoids the Puncovní úřad (hallmarking office); no rattles, which would be toys needing EN 71.
  - Price and terms: **390–690 CZK per kit excl. VAT**, minimum order 10, with a one-click reorder.
  - **Line 2:** "first written mention" anniversary packs (650/700/750 years): crest enamel pins, engraved mugs and a plaque, sold 12–18 months ahead of the date.
  - **Line 3:** the same packs for volunteer fire brigade (SDH) anniversaries.
- **Why demand > supply.** This is a distribution gap, not scarcity, and the file should say so honestly.
  - There are about 6,250 municipalities, most of them small with part-time mayors [knowledge].
  - Small-scale purchases need no tender [knowledge; verify the current threshold].
  - The need repeats with every birth: roughly 77–85k births a year [knowledge], giving a market of about 60k children × ~400 CZK ≈ 24M CZK/yr [estimate].
  - These buyers don't shop on Etsy, so marketplace sellers never reach them.
  - Anniversary demand can be predicted years ahead from each municipality's first-mention year.
- **CZ edge.** Only Czech buyers. The data is public:
  - crests in REKOS (the parliamentary register of municipal symbols);
  - municipal e-mail addresses on municipal sites and in the databox register;
  - first-mention years on Wikipedia and in ČSÚ data.
- **Economics [estimate].**
  - Unit economics: COGS 120–220 CZK (engraved box 50–90, print 15, engraved spoon 40–70, packaging 20). At 490 CZK the gross margin is about 60%. A 15-kit order brings about 4,400 CZK of gross profit.
  - Scenarios:

    | Case | Municipalities | Orders/yr each | Profit |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Pessimistic | 10 | 1.5 | ≈ €150/month |
    | Realistic | 30 (0.5% of all) | 1.5 | ≈ 200k CZK/yr ≈ **€650/month** |
    | Optimistic | 80 | 1.5 | ≈ €1,700/month |

  - Capex: a laser engraver at €800–2,500, or a partner engraver.
  - Time: 3–5 min per kit, plus outreach.
  - The sales cycle follows municipal budgets, which are approved in December. Pitch in October–November.
- **Legal.** § 34a of the Municipalities Act: others may use municipal symbols only with the municipality's consent, so put the consent in the order. GPSR applies to the product. EKO-KOM (packaging EPR) applies.
- **Kill if** crest-personalised kits at a 10-piece minimum are already sold widely, or mayors only buy locally.
- **Queries:**
  1. `vítání občánků dárky pro obce se znakem obce e-shop`
  2. `"vítání občánků" dárek miminku obec cena pamětní list lžička 2025`

### N2: Honeybee nucleus colonies (*oddělky*) and mated queens, pre-sold with deposits (type S: biology)
- **Offer.**
  - 5-frame nucs on the Czech 39×24 frame standard, each with a current-year queen, at **3,500–4,500 CZK**. Pre-sold Dec–Mar with a 1,000 CZK deposit, collected May–June.
  - Mated queens at 400–650 CZK, Jun–Aug.
  - Honey as a by-product, sold directly.
- **Why demand > supply.** Supply can't respond within a season. A nuc needs a strong overwintered colony plus about 6 weeks.
  - Every winter, varroa-driven losses of roughly 10–30% [knowledge; verify] create replacement demand at the moment everyone wants to buy.
  - New beekeepers need starter colonies at the same time.
  - The price is anchored by replacement need, not by fashion.
- **CZ edge.** One of Europe's densest beekeeping cultures, at about 60k beekeepers and 700k colonies [knowledge]. Beekeepers' association (ČSV) courses and local branches. Collection is local, so no live animals are shipped. Colony registration and the vet movement certificate are routine [knowledge]. Colony and equipment subsidies may exist [knowledge; verify].
- **Economics [estimate].**
  - Capex ≈ 85k CZK (≈ €3.5k): 12 hives ≈ 30k, 8 starter colonies ≈ 32k, equipment ≈ 15k, treatment and sugar ≈ 8k.
  - Year 1 (2027) builds to 16–20 colonies and sells about 10 nucs plus 100 kg of honey, or **€150–250/month** averaged.
  - Year 2, from 20 production colonies: 30 nucs × 3,800 + 20 queens × 500 + 250 kg honey × 200 ≈ 174k CZK revenue, about 130k profit ≈ **€440/month**.
  - Time: 6–8 h/week on average, with a peak of 12–15 h in Apr–Jun.
- **Kill risks.**
  - The learning curve is at least one season, so this is strongest if the founder already keeps bees.
  - American foulbrood (AFB) quarantine zones ban moving colonies.
  - It needs an apiary site.
  - The founder has to buy scarce nucs in year 1.
- **Queries:**
  1. `oddělky 39x24 prodej 2026 cena vyprodáno objednávky`
  2. `zimní úhyn včelstev 2025/2026 Česko procenta`
  3. `včelí matky prodej 2026 cena oplozená matka čekací doba`

### N3: Heritage-variety fruit trees, sold as 1-year maidens and later as high-stems for gardeners and subsidised municipal orchards and alleys (type S: biology)
- **Offer.**
  - Bench-grafted 1-year maidens of old Czech and Central-European apple, pear and plum varieties (*krajové odrůdy*), bare-root, at **290–450 CZK**, sold by pre-order list in October–November and March–April.
  - From year 3: 2–3-year high-stems (*vysokokmeny*) at 700–1,200 CZK for municipalities and NGOs planting landscape orchards and alleys.
- **Why demand > supply.** Supply lags demand by 1–3 years of biology. High-stem old-variety stock comes from few nurseries. Demand comes from two sources:
  - the heritage-garden trend;
  - state-funded landscape planting [knowledge; verify the current programmes and calls].

  "Specialist nurseries sell out" is **unverified**.
- **CZ edge.** A Czech-language gardener market, knowledge of Czech varieties, cheap land (a 300–600 m² garden or chalupa plot), and scion wood from old trees.
- **Economics [estimate].**
  - 800 grafts × 80% take = 640 maidens. Selling 550 × 330 CZK ≈ 181k CZK.
  - Costs: rootstocks ≈ 18k, sundries ≈ 12k, packaging ≈ 10k.
  - Result: ≈ 140k CZK/yr ≈ **€470/month** at 4–6 h/week on average.
  - The first sale comes only in autumn 2027, roughly month 12.
- **Legal (the gate check).**
  - A plant passport (EU 2016/2031) and ÚKZÚZ registration as a professional operator.
  - The fruit propagating-material rules (Dir. 2008/90/EC) require registered varieties or an officially recognised description. **This may block unregistered landraces.**
  - Fire-blight protected-zone rules may restrict some destinations.
- **Queries:**
  1. `staré krajové odrůdy jabloní prodej štěpy vyprodáno podzim 2025`
  2. `vysokokmeny ovocných stromů nedostatek školky výsadba alejí dotace 2026`

## 4. Approaches (methods, not products)

- **A1: Mine "Koupím" ads.** Bazoš and Aukro wanted ads measure unmet demand directly. For each category, count wanted ads against offers. Query: `site:bazos.cz koupím <category>`, and compare with the for-sale count.
- **A2: Sell into want-list marketplaces rather than list-and-hope ones.** Discogs, BrickLink, Numista and Colnect notify buyers when a wanted item is listed. Asking prices then matter less, because sold prices and want counts are public (N5).
- **A3: Make a deposit-backed pre-sale the standard test.** A kill gate such as "≥30 deposits in 4 weeks" measures demand. Listing counts measure supply. Apply this to N2, N3 and N6, and retro-fit it to C15.
- **A4: Screen for import gaps.** Compare Allegro.pl or Kaufland.de bestsellers with Heureka.cz, looking for "0 obchodů" (no shops) or only foreign sellers. Then filter for bulky, dangerous-goods or regulated items, where foreign sellers stay away. Query: `site:heureka.cz "<product>"` for each of the top 50 Allegro.pl hobby bestsellers.
- **A5: Use buyer lenses.** Look at municipalities, SDH (about 7k units), folk ensembles, hunting associations and schools. Orders repeat, there are no marketplace fees, and few sellers target them.
- **A6: Pass cheap regulatory gates that deter casual sellers.** The ÚKZÚZ plant passport, Puncovní úřad notification and ČSV colony registration cost a determined founder little and keep hobbyists out.
- **A7: Take a skills and network inventory of the founder before the next round.** The founder's employer's industry may itself be the best B2B channel.

## 5. Critique of the current top list

**Over-rated**
- **C02 (#1, 68.0).** Its asymmetry is in price, not in demand. The dossier itself says "sell-through is binding". Consensus formed because the downside is small, not because the upside is asymmetric. It's a fine hobby business, but it shouldn't be presented as the asymmetric pick.
- **C07, C01, C06.** These have the same shape: skilled full-time pickers and modders compete for the same donors, and a 10–15 h/week founder loses that race.
- **C12, C03, C05, C04.** The scores rested on spreads or gaps that verification then showed were already arbitraged, legally tainted, or filled. The scoring pipeline rewarded evidence that is easy to find, and that evidence mostly shows supply.

**Under-rated**
- **C15 (hand-dyed yarn advent calendar, 47.5, not deep-dived).** It is the only one of the 35 with pre-order *sold-out* evidence and a model that sells before making. It was cut because it needs a skill and is seasonal, which is exactly the bias described in §1.4. It deserves a deep dive ahead of C08 and C10. The key checks are EU sell-outs and whether the 8+ Czech dyers already sell calendars.
- **C11 (TM31 end-of-support parts, 48.5).** It is the only candidate with an OEM supply cut. Third-party sellers (electropapa, krempl, sos-parts) are filling the gap, but it is still more structural than any survivor. It is worth a quick check on which specific parts nobody reproduces.

**Structural suggestion for the next round**
- Rank candidates first by **how the supply constraint works**: biology, licence, craft capacity routed around by partnering, or finite stock sold into a want list.
- Rank second by **directly measured demand**: deposits, want counts, "koupím" ratios, waitlist lengths.
- Rank last by listing counts.
- Drop anything whose profit is capped by the founder's own hours × €15/h, unless it is only a cheap first step.
